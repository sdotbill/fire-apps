# Growth Audit — Maki Tree Service (Davison, MI)
**Prepared by Premium Marketing — November 25, 2026**

This audit sets the baseline for the website rebuild and search work now under way. It follows up the October 3, 2026 audit. What changed since then: a Facebook business page was located (dormant since April 2026). What has not changed: the hours still disagree across Google, the website, and Yelp; the review count sits where it was; and the website has not been updated since 2024.

We looked at how Maki Tree Service shows up online — Google, the company website, Facebook, Instagram, and reviews. This is a real, established company: in business since 1997 (per the Better Business Bureau and Yelp), A+ BBB rating, licensed and insured, serving Genesee and Lapeer Counties from 10047 East Richfield Road, Davison, MI. Three things stood out as costing them customers right now:

### 1. Customers in Flint and Lapeer never see her
**What we found:** Maki Tree Service ranks first for "tree service Davison MI" and first for "tree removal Davison MI" (checked November 25, 2026). But for the bigger markets she serves, she does not appear in the top results: "tree service Flint MI," "tree removal Flint MI," "tree service Lapeer MI," and "tree removal Lapeer MI" (all checked November 25, 2026) show other companies, not Maki. The companies at the top of the Flint searches are J&M Tree Service (Flint, jmtreeservice.com) and, on the review-count lists, Above All Tree Services and Hauling (5.0 stars, 86 reviews) and The Tree Guy of Clio (4.8 stars, 166 reviews). In Lapeer, the top spots belong to Lapeer-based companies: JDM Tree & Site Services LLC (4.9 stars, 161 reviews) and Legend Tree Service LLC (4.9 stars, 147 reviews). Bill reports she comes up second organically for "tree service in Flint"; our independent checks on November 25, 2026 did not show her in the top 10 for Flint terms, so that position could not be confirmed from outside — rankings vary by the searcher's location and by whether the search shows the map or the web results.
**Why it costs you:** Davison is her home town and her smallest market. Flint is the county seat and the biggest pool of customers, and Lapeer is the second county she serves. A homeowner in Flint or Lapeer searching for tree removal never gets to her name, so the call goes to the company at the top of the list. Being first in Davison does not cover the two markets where most of the work is.
**What fixing it looks like:** A rebuilt website with a page for each service in each town — a tree removal page for Flint, a tree removal page for Lapeer, and the same for trimming, stump grinding, and the rest — each one indexed properly so Google has something to rank when someone in those towns searches. That is the work now under way.

### 2. The website is four pages, untouched since 2024
**What we found:** The website, makitreeservice.com, has four content pages: Home, About Us, Our Services, and Contact Us (sitemap checked November 25, 2026). The service pages were last modified in June 2024 and the contact page in August 2024 — more than two years ago. There is no page for Flint, no page for Lapeer, no page for tree removal, no page for stump grinding, and no page for any single service. One generic services page covers everything. The site is built on a template (Ad Local) and the homepage carries three customer testimonials and both phone numbers, which is good — but Google has almost nothing to rank for the searches in Issue 1.
**Why it costs you:** Google ranks pages, not companies. With no page about tree removal in Flint, there is nothing for Google to show a Flint homeowner searching for tree removal. And a homeowner who does land on the site sees a page that has not changed in two years, which does not inspire confidence next to a competitor whose site shows current work and current information.
**What fixing it looks like:** A full rebuild: a page per service per town, current photos of real jobs, clear phone numbers and a free-estimate form on every page, and the whole site indexed properly. Then the Flint and Lapeer pages have a real chance to rank.

### 3. No follow-up system — reviews go unanswered, social media is dormant, and the hours still disagree
**What we found:** Google reviews stand at 4.7 stars across 53 reviews (Birdeye snapshot, checked November 25, 2026) — the same count as the October audit, which means no new Google reviews have landed in the meantime. The one negative review visible (a customer who was quoted over $1,000 more than other companies and felt dismissed when asking about a veterans discount) has no reply from the business. Yelp is claimed and carries 27 business photos, but has zero reviews. The Facebook business page ("Maki tree service") has 26 followers and its last post was a crew video on April 3, 2026 — more than seven months ago. Business posts instead appear on the owner's personal Facebook profile (last business post August 24, 2026, one or two likes each). There is no Instagram account for the business. And the hours are still listed three different ways, the same conflict the October audit flagged: Google shows 9:00 AM–5:00 PM every day including Sunday; the website says Sunday closed with weekday hours of 6:00 AM–7:00 PM (Friday until 9:00 PM); Yelp and Birdeye show Monday–Saturday 8:00 AM–5:00 PM, Sunday closed (all checked November 25, 2026).
**Why it costs you:** Tree work is high-trust and high-ticket, and homeowners compare. A competitor in Lapeer shows 147 to 359 reviews and posts job videos every week; Maki shows 53 reviews, a silent Facebook page, and no Instagram. The unanswered negative review sits there for every comparison shopper to read. And the conflicting hours mean a homeowner with a storm-damaged tree on a Sunday morning gets a different answer depending on where they look — and calls the next company instead.
**What fixing it looks like:** One set of real hours, made identical on Google, the website, and every directory. A simple habit after every finished job: a text to the customer with a direct link to leave a Google review. A reply to every review, starting with the negative one. And the Facebook page fed with phone photos from the crew after each job — before, during, after — instead of business posts living on a personal profile.
