Sleep, Bedding and the Night Room

The room people sleep in: beds, blankets, light, air and noise through the night, and what each costs or protects.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119402

Bed Height for Older Sleepers: The Seat-Height Rule That Minimises Sit-to-Stand Effort, and the Fall Energy Cost of Exceeding It

The result. For a popliteal height of 42 cm, standing from a 45 cm bed top requires an effort index of 103.7, against 100.0 at the recommended height and 57.1 from a fashionable 65 cm top — the effort curve is steep below knee height and flat above it. Raising the top to 75 cm raises the energy of a roll-out fall for a 70 kg sleeper from 316 J at the recommended height to 515 J.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119435

Blackout Is a Gap Problem: Light at the Eye from Blind Edge Leakage as a Function of Gap Width, and the Overlap That Replaces Expensive Fabric

The result. With the gaps sealed, blackout fabric holds the eye to 0.15 lux; a gap of only 2.0 mm around the perimeter breaches a stated 1 lux design criterion, and a 10 mm gap admits 4.6 lux — more than an ordinary curtain with sealed edges delivers through its fabric at 7.5 lux. A simple overlap strip that forces gap light through two reflections cuts the 10 mm case to 0.32 lux.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119453

How Much Blanket Is Enough: A Tog Sizing Rule from Sleeping Heat Production, Room Temperature, and the Insulation Already Supplied by the Mattress

The result. The requirement falls from 15.5 tog at 10 degrees to 8.0 tog at 19 degrees and 5.5 tog at 22 degrees. A 13.5 tog duvet in a 19 degree room therefore carries 5.5 tog of surplus insulation, which in the model would drive mean skin temperature to 40.1 degrees if the sleeper did not respond by throwing it off.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119468

Dawn Simulation, Priced in Lux-Minutes: The Ramp Duration and Peak Illuminance a Wake-Up Light Needs at Realistic Pillow Distances

The result. At the bedside the peak is 300.0 lux and a 30-minute ramp delivers 4500 lux-minutes; across the room at 1.8 metres the peak collapses to 23.1 lux and the same ramp delivers 347 lux-minutes, far below a stated effective band of 2500 to 7500. Reaching the band's middle from across the room would need a ramp of 432.0 minutes.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119508

Soft Infant Sleep Surfaces and Exhaled-Air Pooling: A Rebreathing Model That Converts Surface Firmness into a Carbon Dioxide Fraction at the Face

The result. With stated pocket volumes of 120 millilitres on a soft surface against 25 on a firm one, the steady inspired carbon dioxide fraction reaches 4.4 percent on the soft surface, against 0.04 percent — effectively fresh air — on the firm surface, while inspired oxygen falls to 16.6 percent. Firmness, converted into pocket geometry, becomes a number a parent can reason with.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119528

Mattress Firmness as a Pressure Problem: Peak Interface Pressure Across Body Masses, and the Mass Above Which No Uniform Surface Stays Below the Capillary Threshold

The result. Peak pressure rises steadily with stiffness — at 75 kg, from 3.97 kPa on the softest layer to 6.38 kPa on the firmest — while sink depth falls. Scoring both failures against a capillary threshold of 4.27 kPa and a 110 mm support limit, a stiffness window exists for the 55 kg sleeper (40, 60, 80, 100 kPa per metre) and a narrow one for the 75 kg sleeper (40), but none at all for the 95 kg sleeper, whose best achievable peak is 4.8 kPa even before the support limit is applied.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119548

When Is a Mattress Finished: A Sag-Growth Model and a Measurable Replacement Rule Based on the Depth That Matters to the Spine

The result. Foam reaches the stated 25 mm sag criterion at 6.6 years and the 2.5 degree misalignment criterion at 18.4 years; innerspring reaches the sag criterion at 16.4 years and does not reach the alignment criterion within the thirty-year horizon modelled, standing at 1.21 degrees at year ten. At ten years the two constructions carry 29.8 mm and 19.3 mm of sag respectively.

How Far a Toss Travels: Motion Transfer Across a Shared Mattress as a Function of Distance, and the Separation at Which a Partner's Turn Falls Below Perception

The result. Against a stated perception threshold of 0.8 mm, the disturbance stays perceptible for 1.39 m across a coil mattress, 0.71 m across pocket springs, and 0.4 m across foam. At the 0.85 m centre separation of a queen bed, the coil mattress still delivers 2.13 mm to the partner while foam delivers 0.05 mm.

The Mosquito Net Trade-Off: Mesh Aperture Against Air Movement, and the Hole Size at Which a Net Stops Cooling the Sleeper It Protects

The result. At the 1.2 mm aperture that excludes the malaria mosquito, the open fraction is 0.685 and the air speed under the net falls to 0.567 of the unnetted value, with relative cooling at 0.711. The 0.6 mm mesh needed against sandflies cuts air speed to 0.352 and cooling to 0.534.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119598

The Closed Bedroom Door: Overnight Carbon Dioxide Accumulation for One and Two Sleepers, and the Door Gap or Vent Area That Holds It Below 1000 ppm

The result. Two sleepers behind a closed door end the night at 4777 ppm, heading for a steady state of 8332 ppm, and spend 7.2 hours above 1000 ppm; one sleeper ends at 2598 ppm. Holding two sleepers at or below 1000 ppm requires 1.36 air changes per hour — a flow of 13.8 litres per second.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119610

Bedroom Humidity Against Dust Mites: The Ventilation and Temperature Conditions That Hold a Bedroom Below the Mite Breeding Threshold Through a Winter Night

The result. At the closed-door infiltration rate of 0.1 air changes per hour the room settles at 100.0 percent, deep inside breeding territory; holding it at or below a stated 55 percent target requires 0.27 air changes per hour. The same calculation exposes a winter constraint in the other direction: single glazing condenses once room humidity passes 61.3 percent.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119620

The Bathroom Trip at 3 a.m.: How Bright, and in What Colour, a Night Light Can Be Before Its Melanopic Dose Rivals the Evening Screen

The result. A low amber fitting delivers 2.2 melanopic lux-minutes, 0.09 percent of the evening's dose; a bright daylight-white fitting delivers 114.0, 4.8 percent of the evening dose in four minutes. Inverting the calculation gives a design bound: to keep a single trip under two percent of the evening dose, light at the eye must stay below 12.0 melanopic lux — 66.7 photopic lux if amber, but only 12.6 lux if daylight-white.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119629

Sizing Earplug Attenuation for Street-Facing Bedrooms: The Reduction Actually Required to Bring Passing-Traffic Peaks Below the Sleep-Disturbance Criterion

The result. A foam plug labelled 32 dB delivers 17.6 dB in the model, which is ample for the arterial case, leaving peaks of 40.4 dBA, but on an urban main road the requirement is 23.0 dB and the same plug leaves 50.4 dBA — above criterion. The practical output is a three-line sizing table, and a clear statement of the exposure level at which plugs alone stop being the answer and the window or the room must change instead.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119641

A Pillow-Loft Rule from Neck Geometry: Compressed Loft as a Function of Shoulder Width, Head Mass, and Fill Stiffness for Side and Back Sleeping

The result. A standard 15 cm down pillow leaves that sleeper's neck tilted 28.0 degrees from neutral. The deliverable is a two-measurement rule — shoulder breadth and fill compression — that replaces firmness adjectives with arithmetic.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119650

What a Sleeping Bag Rating Actually Buys: Insulation Thickness Required per Degree Below Freezing, and the Cost of the Mattress Pad the Rating Assumes

The result. The loft needed over the body is 7.4 cm at 10 degrees, 10.8 cm at freezing, 14.1 cm at minus 10, and 17.4 cm at minus 20 — about 1.7 cm of additional loft for every five degrees. Beneath the body the bag's loft compresses nearly flat, and the paper computes the pad resistance that must replace it: at minus 10 degrees the pad must supply at least 1.04 square-metre kelvin per watt, without which the underside alone would drain 218.0 watts per square metre.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24119661

The Weighted Blanket Claim, Weighed: Interface Pressure from a 10 Percent Body-Weight Blanket Against the Pressures Its Marketing Invokes

The result. A blanket of 7.0 kg for a 70 kg sleeper, resting on a stated 0.85 square metres of body surface, produces a mean interface pressure of 0.08 kPa and a modelled peak of 0.18 kPa over bony areas — 2 percent of the 4.27 kPa capillary threshold, and far below the pressures of the hugs and compression garments the advertising evokes. Reaching even 3 kPa of mean pressure would require a blanket of 259.9 kg, which no sleeper could lift onto the bed.
