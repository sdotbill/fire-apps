Clay, Pottery and Kilns

Clay dug, washed, shaped and fired: kilns, wheels, glazes and drying, each step costed in fuel, time and rejects.

How Much Insulation Pays in a Small Brick Kiln: A Thickness Rule Balancing Fuel Saved Against Blanket Cost

The result. Under the stated assumptions, 38 mm of ceramic fibre blanket on a 12 m2 kiln wall minimises total annual cost at USD 916.0, saving USD 466.4 per year against the uninsulated kiln (USD 1382.4) and cutting fuel per firing from 432.0 kg to 181.8 kg of wood.

Chimney Height for a Downdraft Pottery Kiln: The Draft Pressure Needed and the Height Beyond Which Fuel Use Barely Falls

The result. A 3 m chimney gives the lowest fuel use in the sweep at 427.2 kg per firing (draft 22.3 Pa), 5.7 percent below a 2 m chimney (453.0 kg), while a 10 m chimney wastes fuel on excess draft at 514.6 kg per firing.

Covered Drying for Green Bricks: How Much Roof per Thousand Bricks Keeps Rain Losses Below Fuel Savings

The result. Covering 60 percent of the drying yard minimises cost per batch of 10,000 bricks at USD 152.5, cutting rain losses from 2,200 bricks uncovered to 628, against USD 198.0 uncovered and USD 178.0 for full cover.

Ceramic Water Filter Wall Thickness: The Flow and Pathogen-Removal Trade-Off and a Thickness That Meets Both Targets

The result. A 12 mm filter wall is the thinnest section meeting both stated targets at once, flowing 1.67 litres per hour with a log removal value of 3.36; a 6 mm wall flows 3.33 litres per hour but removes only 1.68 log, and an 18 mm wall reaches 5.04 log at 1.11 litres per hour.

Wet Fuelwood and Kiln Fuel Bills: The True Fuel Mass Multiplier at Common Moisture Contents, Against the Claim That Wet Wood Burns Almost as Well

The result. The claim that wet wood burns almost as well as dry wood is disproved by evaporation arithmetic: effective heat falls from 15.94 MJ per kg at 10 percent moisture to 5.64 MJ per kg at 60 percent, so fuel mass must rise by a factor of 2.07 at 50 percent moisture and 1.35 at 30 percent to deliver the same heat.

A Crazing-Free Window for Low-Cost Glazes: The Thermal Expansion Mismatch a Fired Glaze Can Tolerate on a Porous Earthenware Body

The result. The crazing threshold for the stated glaze is an expansion mismatch of 0.75 parts per million per degree C: at a mismatch of +1.0 the glaze stress reaches 46.7 MPa, above the 35 MPa tensile strength, while +0.5 gives 23.3 MPa and is the largest safe positive mismatch in the sweep.

Rice Husk in Fired Bricks: Why More Husk Is Not Always Better, and the Blend That Best Balances Strength, Weight and Cost

The result. The claim that more rice husk always improves a brick does not survive the arithmetic: the scored optimum is a 0.10 husk fraction (strength 11.1 MPa), while at 0.30 the strength falls to 5.2 MPa, below the 7 MPa loadbearing threshold, even though density falls to 1152 kg per cubic metre.

Flywheel Sizing for a Human-Powered Potter's Kick Wheel: The Rim Mass That Holds Speed Within a Usable Band While Centring Clay

The result. A 35 kg flywheel rim is the lightest that holds speed droop to 21.3 percent during a centring episode, against 49.7 percent at 15 kg; a 100 kg rim stores 809 J against 283 J at 35 kg, with correspondingly heavier starting effort.

Sizing a Settling Tank for Washed Clay: The Residence Time That Drops Sand While Keeping Fine Clay in Suspension

The result. A settling residence time of 2 hours minimises combined loss: sand remaining falls to 0.0045 of its initial fraction (against 0.2592 at half an hour), while extending settling to 8 hours loses 0.086 of the fine clay fraction for little further gain.

Cooking Pot Wall Thickness and Thermal Shock: A Lower Bound on Survival Over an Open Flame and an Upper Bound Set by Heating Time

The result. Cooking pot walls have a survival window of 4 to 6 mm under the stated assumptions: at 3 mm the mechanical factor of safety is only 1.01, at 10 mm the thermal factor of safety falls to 0.89, and heating time at the top of the window is 24.6 minutes.

Washing Clay to Prevent Efflorescence: The Number of Wash Cycles That Brings Soluble Salt Below a Visible-Bloom Threshold

The result. Three wash cycles first bring residual soluble salt below the stated 0.08 percent visible-bloom threshold, from an initial 0.85 percent (0.104 percent after two cycles), using 1200 litres of water per tonne of clay.

Sand Temper and Drying Cracks: The Temper Fraction That Minimises Combined Crack and Strength Losses in Hand-Formed Ware

The result. A sand temper fraction of 0.25 by volume minimises combined drying-crack and strength losses at USD 117.9 per 1,000 pieces, against USD 372.0 untempered, with a crack loss of 0.077 and fired strength of 11.4 MPa at the optimum.

Soak Time at Peak Temperature: The Hold Duration That Minimises the Combined Cost of Fuel and Underfired Rejects

The result. A 3 hour soak at peak temperature minimises combined cost at USD 35.0 per firing, against USD 270.0 with no soak; the reject fraction falls from 0.300 with no soak to 0.024 at the optimum, and a 4 hour soak already costs more (USD 36.5).

How Tightly a Kiln Can Be Stacked: A Load Density Limit Set by Temperature Spread Across the Setting

The result. Profit is maximised at a stacking density of 0.55 of kiln volume (USD 305.3 per firing, 937 good pieces, temperature spread 152.7 degrees C); packing to 0.75 widens the spread to 263.0 degrees C and lowers profit to USD 219.7 despite loading more ware.

Sun Pre-Drying Bricks Before Firing: The Moisture Content at Which Further Drying Stops Paying in Saved Kiln Fuel

The result. Drying ware to 6 percent moisture before firing minimises total cost at USD 53.5 per firing, against USD 86.1 at 18 percent moisture (of which USD 23.8 is fuel alone) and USD 54.2 at 4 percent, where extra yard days outweigh the remaining fuel saving.

Setter Spacing for Flat Roof Tiles: The Support Interval That Keeps Firing Warpage Within a Stated Tolerance

The result. Setter supports may be spaced up to 300 mm apart while keeping retained firing bow within the 3 mm tolerance; at 400 mm the bow reaches 5.45 mm, against 0.34 mm at 200 mm spacing.

A Span Limit for Self-Supporting Brick Kiln Vaults: The Width a Catenary Arch of Fired Brick Can Cover Without Steel

The result. A standard 0.46 m two-brick abutment wall supports a catenary brick vault up to a 4.0 m span (required abutment 0.441 m, thrust 4.67 kN per metre); at 5.0 m the required abutment rises to 0.551 m and exceeds what the standard wall provides.
