Drainage Physics for Mosquito Breeding Sites Near Schools: A Grading and Drain Specification That Keeps Standing Water Below the Aquatic Development Time, Priced per School

The problem. Mosquito breeding in puddles, ruts, blocked drains and borrow pits near schools that hold water longer than the 7-day warm-season aquatic development time.

The result. Grading to a 10 mm residual (slopes 0.5–2.0%, drains at 50–20 m spacing by soil class) cuts the seasonal probability of a 7-day standing spell to zero on sand through clay loam at $1,124.40–$1,254.00 per 1 ha school, while piped clay drainage ($6,681.00/10 yr) honestly loses to larviciding ($3,536.00/10 yr).

Cookstove Burn Prevention by Geometry and Shielding: A Reach-Envelope Design Rule for Children Aged 1–3 Years

The result. Elevation and setback alone do not defeat a tall 3-year-old's touch (90 cm stove needs 56 cm setback); a fixed shield plus setback does (e.g. 90 cm + 20 cm setback + 20 cm shield), and pot weight is no safeguard (pots slide at 5.9–19.6 N, tip at 10.8–36.0 N). Proven in simulation.

The result. Over six years per child, nets prevent 9,182 bites at $0.62 per 1,000 bites prevented, screening prevents 9,625 at $6.44 per 1,000, and both together prevent 12,964 at $5.22 per 1,000 after charging screening's ventilation penalty (ratio 0.54, +1.24 °C, net use 0.75 to 0.57); the price ranking flips only jointly or where screening cannot be installed.

School Latrine Ventilation: A Sizing Bound for Ventilated Improved Pit Latrines from Wind and Solar Airflow Physics

The result. A 150 mm matte-black vent pipe, 3.2 m tall, with a static cowl and clean 1.2 mm fly screen meets the 0.20 m/s down-hole odour criterion (32.4 m³/h, 10.5 air changes per hour) in 99.5% of 1,080 simulated school hours at US$0.282 per pupil per year, while a 110 mm pipe meets it in only 61.3% and screen fouling cuts the 150 mm design to 69.1% (moderate) and 28.5% (severe); calm overcast hours fail at 150 mm and flooded pits fail at any diameter. The result. School Meal Cold Chain: A Phase-Change Box That Holds Milk and Cooked Protein at or Below 5 °C for a Six-Hour Kitchen-to-School Journey

The problem. Perishable school meal components (milk, cooked protein) warming above safe temperature during unrefrigerated transport and waiting between a central kitchen and a school.

The result. A 40 mm EPS box with 6 kg of 2 °C PCM holds milk and cooked protein for 60 children at 2.0 °C through 8 h at 32 °C ambient (5 °C crossing at 11.20 h vs 1.32 h without PCM), needs two freezers (not one) to re-freeze a 500-child fleet overnight, and costs $1.15 per child per year; half-frozen panels fail at 6.53 h, and at 4.97 h if the box is also opened 30 times.

Vitamin A Loss Kinetics in Stored Fortified Food: A Storage Bound for School Feeding Programmes

The result. Fortified cooking oil kept dark and sealed falls below 70% of its nominal vitamin A level in 47.9 days at 30 °C and 32.5 days at 35 °C; a stated 90-day mixed supply chain retains 36.9% and needs a 2.71× overage (US$0.0231 per child per year in active ingredient) to guarantee the full dose, while clear containers, daily opening and 180-day tropical chains fail at any permissible overage.

School Water Points Sized by Queue Physics: How Many Taps a School Needs So That Filling a Container Fits Inside a Twenty-Minute Break

The result. A 300-pupil school needs 2 taps at 6 L/min (not 1) for 95% of seekers to finish filling within a 20-minute break; the added tap saves a simulated 511,670 pupil-minutes per year, while a single tap serves only 66.5% and a mid-break flow collapse drops the sized design to 53.8%. Proven in simulation.
