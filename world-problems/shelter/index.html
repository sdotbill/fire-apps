Shelter and infrastructure

Houses that stand up and warn before they fail: earth blocks any soil can make, sanitation without sewers, roofs tied down, foundations that float, and cheap witnesses on bridges, slopes and cracks.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108427

A Low-Cost Bridge-Scour Witness Instrument: A Tilt-Node Chain Design and Its Computed Detection Budget

The problem. Riverbed scour around bridge piers can deepen unseen between inspections, and a cheap continuous witness can report promptly when the bed falls past a chosen depth.

The result. A chain of 16 tilt nodes spaced 0.20 m apart is computed to witness pier scour to within 0.20 m over a 3.2 m range, detect a new exposure with probability 0.892 within one hour at a model false-alarm rate of 0.0110 per chain per year, run a computed 9.32 years on one battery pack (service capped at 5.0 years), and cost $191.40 in parts.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108442

A Universal 6% Equal-Split Lime-Cement Formulation for Stabilised-Earth Wall Blocks: A Mix-Selection Computation Balancing Strength, Wet Durability, Embodied Energy and Cost

The problem. Durable house walls are too expensive and energy-intensive for low-income builders who have suitable soil on site but no laboratory to choose a block mix.

The result. One recipe — 94% local soil with 3% cement and 3% lime — works in simulation across soils from 15% to 40% clay and cuts the wall's embodied energy by 23.8% compared with an 8% cement-only block.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108452

A Sewerless Thermal Sanitation Unit for a Household: Computed Pathogen Lethality, Energy per Use, and Safe-Handling Intervals for a 2-Litre Batch Design

The problem. Households without a sewer connection must store and empty faecal material that can carry bacteria, viruses, and helminth eggs.

The result. A 2.0 kg household batch held at 65 °C for 25 min gives a computed 7.41-log reduction of Ascaris suum eggs (the limiting organism; 6-log needs 20.24 min) using 0.1188 kWh per use, with safe handling after 11.06 h sealed or 1.11 h vented to 40 °C, in a unit costing US$118.00 in parts (Proven in simulation).

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108520

A Passive Radiative-Cooling Roof Assembly for Heat-Wave Housing: Computed Cooling Budget and Price per Degree of Indoor Temperature Reduction

The problem. Dangerous indoor overheating during heat waves in lightweight, poorly insulated housing whose occupants have no air conditioning.

The result. The computed assembly lowers the daily-mean indoor temperature of a vulnerable 16 m² room by 3.61–3.75 K versus a dark roof at USD 76.75–79.80 per degree, but in humid heat its sub-ambient cooling at peak sun falls to effectively zero (4.8 W/m², 0.4 K), leaving solar rejection as the only supported benefit.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108637

A Straw and Rice-Husk Fibre Insulation Panel: Specification and Computed Thermal, Moisture, Cost and Fire-Behaviour Envelope

The problem. Homes waste heating fuel for lack of affordable insulation while surplus straw and rice husk are burned or discarded, and a fibre panel is only useful if its moisture and fire limits are stated as plainly as its thermal benefit.

The result. A 100 mm straw/rice-husk panel gives 2.041 m²K/W dry and 1.624 at 80% relative humidity at US$8.42/m², with a 16% moisture design limit, and is combustible with computed ignition in about one second above the critical flux so it may be used only as protected infill behind a non-combustible lining.

A Lashed-Plus-Bolted Bamboo–Timber Joint for Low-Rise Housing: Connector Design and a Computed Shear, Pull-Out and Slip Budget Across Culm Diameter and Moisture

The problem. Low-rise bamboo houses fail at their joints, where lashing alone slips loose and fabricated steel brackets are too expensive or unavailable for the builders who need them.

The result. A single M12 bolt with washer plates plus six turns of rope lashing gives a 120 mm bamboo culm a design shear capacity of 8.29 kN and a pull-out capacity of 7.93 kN at US$4.63 per joint, with every diameter and moisture combination tested in simulation passing.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108696

A Solar Desiccation Sanitation Chamber for Households Without Sewers: Computed Moisture-Removal, Pathogen Die-Off, and Safe-Storage Budgets Across Humidity and Season

The problem. Households without sewers must store and handle wet faecal material that carries disease; drying it with sunlight and storing it dry for a stated interval makes it lighter and safer to handle at no running cost.

The result. A solar drying chamber can dry a household's weekly faecal batch in about a week of ordinary sun, but the dried material still needs about five months of sealed storage before it reaches the stated safe-handling threshold.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108714

A Rainwater-First Roof and Storage System for a Water-Short Household: Integrated Sizing of Roof, First-Flush Diverter and Tank Against a 450 mm Rainfall Regime

The problem. Households in water-short climates size roof, diverter and tank separately, so they run dry in the dry season, spill wet-season rain, or store roof contamination.

The result. A 120.0 m² roof, a 120.0 litre first-flush diverter and a 20,000 litre tank supply a five-person household's 100.0 litres per day on 352.23 days per year (96.50% reliability) in a 450 mm rainfall regime, but reliability falls to 57.12% in drought at 60% rainfall.

A 20% Rice-Husk Ash, 6% Alkali Formulation for Fly-Ash Geopolymer Wall Blocks: A Formulation Search Priced in Strength, Cost and Carbon per Square Metre of Wall

The problem. Durable house walls are unaffordable where cement is costly to buy and transport, while fly ash and rice-husk ash sit unused as waste.

The result. A fly-ash block with 20% rice-husk ash and a 6% alkali dose, cured at 60 °C for 24 hours, reaches a simulated 8.02 MPa at US$17.30 and 24.28 kg CO2e per square metre of wall, but the activator carries 76.7% of the carbon and any dose above 8% is ruled out by the alkali handling limit.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108880

A Porous Ceramic Evaporator Wall for a Single Room in Arid Housing: Computed Indoor Temperature Reduction, Water Use per Degree, and the Humidity Ceiling

The problem. Rooms in arid-climate houses stay dangerously hot where mechanical air conditioning is unaffordable or unsupported, and households cannot judge an evaporative alternative without knowing its cooling, its daily water cost, and the humidity at which it fails.

The result. At 40 degrees Celsius and 20 percent relative humidity outside, the wall holds the room at 29.00 degrees Celsius, a reduction of 11.00 degrees, for 47.21 litres of evaporated water per 12-hour day (4.29 litres per day per degree), and it stops working above 62.7 percent outdoor relative humidity at 40 degrees Celsius.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108914

A Slope-Failure Witness Network for Informal Hillside Settlements: Node Design and a Computed Warning Budget

The problem. Informal hillside settlements stand on steep, marginally stable slopes that fail during heavy rain, often at night, with no instrument on the hillside and no warning at all.

The result. A tilt and soil-moisture witness network is computed to give a hillside settlement 8.14 hours (watch) and 3.90 hours (warning) of lead before a rainfall-driven slope failure in the reference storm (20 mm/h, 1.2 m slip plane), at a nuisance warning rate of 0.192 per slope per year and a parts cost of $22.38 per household.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24109001

A Sacrificial Crack-and-Strain Witness Strip for Small Bridges and Culverts: Design and Computed Threshold Budget

The problem. Cracks in small bridges and culverts can open during an overload, flood, or freeze between inspections and close again leaving no record; an unpowered strip that keeps the peak opening lets an inspector see that the event happened and how large it was.

The result. Eight graded sacrificial ligaments on a passive 100 mm strip are computed to record peak crack opening from 0.20 mm to 1.25 mm in 0.15 mm steps with a worst-case temperature effect of 0.0235 mm against the 0.20 mm first threshold, a governing computed service life of 8.63 years (replacement capped at 5.0 years), and a parts cost of $9.45 per crossing point.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24109079

A Flood-Resilient Amphibious Foundation for a Small House: Buoyancy, Guide-Post Loads and Failure Limits

The problem. Houses on flood-prone land are damaged or destroyed when flood water enters the living space and pushes a fixed structure sideways; occupants lose their home in a single flood.

The result. A 38,000 kg house on a 10.0 m by 8.0 m buoyant base lifts off at 0.50 m of flood water, floats with 0.925 m of freeboard, and carries the design 3.0 m flood at 2.0 m/s with a guide-post bending margin of 1.85, while a guide jammed after 1.00 m of rise fails at a margin of 0.13.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24109101

A Continuous Load-Path Strap-and-Anchor Roof Tie-Down Assembly for Low-Cost Housing: A Computed Uplift Budget and Its Limit Wind Speed

The problem. Hurricane roof loss in low-cost housing caused by a broken uplift load path, where roofs lift off because rafters and walls were never tied down to the foundation.

The result. The computed zoned strap-and-grouted-anchor assembly reaches its design limit at a wind speed of 41.3 m/s (149 km/h), governed by grout-to-masonry anchor bond at 3.67 kN design per unit, against 30.1 m/s for uniform 0.9 m spacing, at USD 264.00 per house.

A Confined-Masonry Wall System for One-Storey Stabilised-Earth Housing: Tie-Column and Ring-Beam Layout with a Computed Seismic Shear and Stability Budget

The problem. Earthquake deaths in low-rise unreinforced masonry housing, where walls separate at corners, fall out of plane, or shear through in plane for want of a dimensioned confinement layout suited to weak, low-cost earth blocks.

The result. For a 7.2 m by 4.8 m one-storey house in 140 mm stabilised-earth block with tie-columns at no more than 2.4 m spacing and a continuous ring beam, the computed design envelope ends at 0.291 g, set by in-plane shear of the short walls, with drift at that point of 0.0419% against a stated 0.40% limit.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24109179

A Passive Solar Heating Wall for Cold-Climate Low-Income Housing: A Computed Sizing Rule, Temperature-Lift Budget, and Cost per Degree

The problem. It attacks cold homes for households that cannot afford heating fuel, by stating before construction what a zero-running-cost solar wall will actually deliver and where it stops being worth building.

The result. An 8.0 m² glazed thermal-mass wall 0.25 m thick lifts a poorly insulated room by 3.45 K at 45° N under a partly cloudy January sky (7.95 K at 35° N clear, 0.64 K at 55° N overcast), delivers 3.368 kWh per night at the central case, is sized by area at 0.37 K per m² with mass thickness held to 0.15–0.25 m, costs $734.49 per degree of lift at the central case, and needs 0.10 m² of vent area to hold an autumn peak of 31.24 °C to 26.20 °C.

A Household Greywater Treatment and Reuse Bed for Water-Short Settlements: A Sizing Rule and Computed Treatment Budget for a Gravel-and-Reed Subsurface-Flow Design

The problem. Water-short households either waste their washing water while gardens go dry or reuse it untreated and spread disease, because they have no correctly sized, low-cost way to treat it.

The result. For five people producing 250 litres of greywater per day, organic loading governs the size at a design area of 6.5 m² (1.30 m² per person), the computed effluent BOD falls from 150 mg/L to 17.0 mg/L at 20 °C, clogging arrives after 3.52 years without a pre-filter and 7.05 years with one, and the treated water may be used only for below-ground irrigation — never for drinking or household contact.
