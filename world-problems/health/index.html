Health without money

Clinics that must work with almost nothing: diagnosis without a CT scanner, oxygen without cylinders, screening with a phone, and instruments whose accuracy limits are printed on their face.

Airborne-Infection Control in a Low-Resource Clinic Waiting Room, Priced per Dollar: A Model-Derived Ranked Protocol

The problem. Low-resource clinic waiting rooms concentrate infectious and susceptible people in poorly ventilated air with almost no budget to fix it.

The result. Wind cross-ventilation buys the most tuberculosis risk reduction per dollar (24.22 percentage points per $100), and pairing its openings with a $180 exhaust fan holds infection probability at 0.0173 even with no wind.

A Low-Dose Chest Radiography Protocol for Tuberculosis Screening Derived from the Dose-Resolution Trade

The problem. Tuberculosis screening images large populations, so the dose per chest film should be set by the TB findings that must be detected rather than inherited from general chest practice.

The result. A quantum-noise model gives a proposed TB screening protocol of 0.0069 mGy per film, 43.48 times below standard practice, preserving cavities, infiltrates and nodules of 5 mm or larger but not miliary nodules.

An Ultrasound-First Diagnostic Pathway for Suspected Appendicitis Where No CT Scanner Exists: A Proposed Decision Protocol with a Cohort Model

The problem. Hospitals without a CT scanner must decide on suspected appendicitis with limited imaging, facing distant transfers, delays, and radiation exposure when CT is eventually used.

The result. In a 1,000-case cohort model, the ultrasound-first protocol settles 52.3% of suspected appendicitis cases by ultrasound alone, avoids 79.57% of transfers and 716.09 mSv of collective dose per 100 patients versus transfer-all-for-CT, at $158.63 per patient, with 5.90 missed cases per 1,000 under the discharge rules and a 17.64% negative-appendectomy implication among settled-positive patients.

A Passive Phase-Change Vaccine Carrier Sized for 72 Hours at 43 °C: Design and Thermal Model

The problem. Vaccines spoil or freeze when power fails for days in hot regions, because passive carriers either run warm too soon or freeze their contents with conventional ice-packs.

The result. With 8.0 kg of 5 °C phase-change material behind a 20 mm vacuum-panel plus 30 mm foam wall, the model holds a 6 kg vaccine payload within 2–8 °C for 88.4 hours at a constant 43 °C ambient, never below 4.23 °C, at an estimated parts cost of US $254.53 (proven in simulation).

Ultraviolet Disinfection for Low-Resource Clinics with Honest Dose Budgets: A Priced Specification for Upper-Room Air Treatment and Surface Terminal Disinfection

The problem. Low-resource clinics are sold ultraviolet disinfection on headline doses that omit distance, shadowing, lamp ageing, and human exposure limits.

The result. Upper-room 254 nm air treatment delivers 12.1 equivalent air changes per hour for tuberculosis at $0.88 per room per day, while occupied far-UVC is capped by exposure limits and surface disinfection cannot reach shadowed sites at any exposure time.

Solar Water Disinfection Made Quantitative: A UV-A Dose Model for a PET Bottle and a Fixed-Geometry Reflector Backing

The problem. Households relying on sunlight to disinfect drinking water have only a rule of thumb, with no dose accounting for place, season, cloud, or water turbidity.

The result. A foil V-trough reflector backing (gain 1.72 clear-sky, $0.96 in materials) cuts time to 3-log E. coli from 1.97 h to 1.23 h on a clear mid-latitude summer day, but a single overcast day remains insufficient for viruses and Giardia cysts with or without it.

Hearing Screening with a Smartphone: An Error Budget and the Protocol It Permits

The problem. Hearing loss often goes unscreened because clinical audiometry requires equipment and a quiet testing room that many people cannot reach.

The result. With model-specific calibration the phone screen supports a binary referral decision at 35 dB HL in a quiet room (PTA error 4.126 dB) but not exact WHO grade assignment, and in a typical room the masking floor removes the normal-to-mild distinction entirely.

Vision Screening with a Smartphone, Bounded by Optics: A Protocol Derived from Display, Camera and Pinhole Limits

The problem. People with treatable vision impairment go unscreened because examination requires equipment and specialists that are not present where they live.

The result. Bounded by its own optics, a smartphone supports a 2 m distance-acuity screen at the 6/12 boundary plus a pinhole step that identifies a refractive cause from 1.19 dioptres, while its built-in photorefraction geometry is blind below 3.33 dioptres and fine acuity grades, dioptre values and astigmatism axis are not supported.

Gravity-Fed Intravenous Infusion Without Pumps, Made Accurate: An Error Budget for the Standard Drip Set and a Constant-Head Mariotte Insert Design

The problem. Gravity IV infusions drift far from their prescribed rate wherever pumps are unavailable, so patients receive fluids late or at the wrong rate.

The result. A standard gravity drip set modelled over 1,000 mL at 125 mL/h finishes 20.8% slow and 12.1% late (22.7% combined error budget), while a US$2.30 Mariotte constant-head insert removes the head-decay term (residual 0.84%) and reaches 9.5% total error only when the clamp is set at the patient after fitting.

A Phase-Change Neonatal Warmer for Births Where No Incubator Exists: Design and Lumped Thermal Model

The problem. Newborns born where no incubator or reliable electricity exists die or are harmed by hypothermia in the first hours of life because they lose heat far faster than they can produce it.

The result. A 2.5 kg phase-change mattress in an insulated wrap holds modelled skin temperature at or above 36.5 °C for at least 6.2 hours for every birth-weight class from 1.0 to 3.5 kg at ambient temperatures of 15 °C and above after one hot-water recharge, and its boiling-water recharge is bounded at a 40 °C pack maximum by a guard phase-change tray.

A Low-Cost Pulse Oximeter with an Honest Accuracy Budget: Design, Error Propagation, and the Accuracy Floor for Triage

The problem. Cheap pulse oximeters are used for triage decisions although their accuracy collapses at low perfusion, in motion, and in dark skin, causing hypoxaemia to be missed.

The result. A US $17.50 discrete-part oximeter design computes to A_rms 1.52 points at perfusion index 3% at rest, 3.37 points in dark pigmentation with a broad emitter, and 2.41 points at the dark low-perfusion corner with narrowband emitters and melanin-stratified calibration — and must blank, not display, readings below perfusion index 1% or above A_rms 3.0 points.

Oxygen for Childhood Pneumonia Where Cylinders Run Out: A Solar Oxygen Concentrator Sized and Priced from Physics

The problem. Children with severe pneumonia die of hypoxaemia in facilities where delivered oxygen cylinders run out.

The result. For a 10-child ward at a mean 1.25 L/min per child, a 4.5 kWp solar array with a 33 kWh battery and three 10 L/min concentrator modules supplies 18.0 m³ of oxygen per day at $0.91 per patient-day, against $18.61 per patient-day for delivered cylinders.

Steam Sterilization of Instruments with a Household Pressure Cooker, Priced from Physics — and an Honest Verdict on Solar Sterilization Claims

The problem. Clinics without an autoclave need to know what proper instrument sterilization costs and whether sunlight can safely replace a controllable heat source.

The result. A validated 121 °C pressure-cooker cycle for 2.0 kg of instruments needs 1.547 MJ and costs $0.061–$0.091 in fuel with 5 cycles per day, while a flat-plate solar collector cannot hold 121 °C at 500 W/m² and is not reliable as a sole-source sterilizer, though evacuated or concentrating collectors of 1–2 m² can complete a cycle in about 1–1.5 sun-hours on a clear day.

Household Smoke from Solid-Fuel Cooking: A Low-Cost Chimney Hood Priced by Draft Physics

The problem. Cooks, mostly women, breathe heavy PM2.5 smoke for hours daily from solid-fuel open fires.

The result. Sized by draft physics (3.5 m, 200 mm flue; 12.02 Pa, 0.0689 m3/s), the chimney hood gives the largest modelled exposure reduction (66.3%) but ranks last per dollar (1.053 %/USD) behind a solar fan (1.411) and an improved stove (1.182), and it fails against adverse wind above about 5.39 m/s.

Passive Cooling for Medicine Storage in a Hot Clinic Without Reliable Power: A Radiative, Evaporative and Thermal-Mass Cabinet Design and Its Computed Climate Envelope

The problem. Clinics in hot regions without reliable power cannot keep medicines labelled for storage at or below 25 °C within their storage class.

The result. In the model the cabinet holds at or below 25 °C through a 40 °C peak day in dry air (maximum 24.95 °C on radiative cooling alone, 23.84 °C with the evaporative stage), holds in 26 of 48 swept climate combinations, and fails in humid heat and at every 45 °C combination.

A Water Witness for Clinics: Design and Accuracy Budget of a Low-Cost Photometer for Free Chlorine and Turbidity at the Point of Care

The problem. Clinics disinfecting or receiving small-supply water often have no affordable way to verify that a chlorine residual persists and that the water is clear enough for disinfection to be trusted.

The result. A $37.90 dual-channel photometer design is computed to detect free chlorine to 0.063 mg/L with about 0.020 mg/L standard uncertainty across the WHO 0.2–0.5 mg/L band and turbidity to 0.159 NTU, sufficient to flag a lost chlorine residual and adjudicate the 5 NTU limit but not to certify samples within about 0.04 mg/L of either chlorine band edge.

A Hand-Powered Centrifuge for Blood Separation Where There Is No Power: A Design Envelope Specified from Rotor Physics

The problem. Clinics without electric power cannot run the centrifuge that hematocrit measurement and concentration-based malaria diagnosis require.

The result. A 100:1 hand-crank design sustaining 6,797 g separates hematocrit samples in a specified 9 minutes and malaria concentration samples in 11 minutes at 48 patients per hour for USD 38.00 in parts, while platelet-poor plasma and quantitative buffy-coat work remain outside the hand-drive envelope.
