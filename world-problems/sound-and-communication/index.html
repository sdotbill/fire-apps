The Bell's Parish: Audibility Radius, Households Reached, and the Bell Mass at Which a Solar Horn Wins on Cost

The result. A 100 kg bell in a 20 m tower is audible over a radius of 1879.6 m and reaches 133.2 households at USD 52.56 per household (19.03 households per USD 1000, the best of the masses tabulated); a USD 650 solar horn passes the bell on households per unit cost only above a crossover bell mass of 2560 kg. Proven in simulation.

The Hand-Crank Siren Indoors: One Roof Siren Against Six Corridor Sounders in a Two-Floor School

The result. One roof hand-crank siren reaches the 75 dBA warning criterion in 24 of 32 rooms (quietest room 70.2 dB) at USD 850 and 90 seconds to full level; six corridor sounders (three per floor) reach all 32 rooms (quietest room 77.6 dB) at USD 770 and 5 seconds — 85 seconds sooner. Proven in simulation.

How Far a Signalling Drum Carries: Range by Terrain, Size and Tension

The result. Reference 0.60 m drum (95 Hz, 100.0 dB at 1 m): maximum range 2.11 km flat open ground, 0.83 km forest, 3.82 km valley; 1.00 m drum reaches 3.57 km flat (1.69x reference); doubled tension shortens flat range to 2.02 km. Proven in simulation.

What Actually Protects Ears: Real-World Attenuation of Foam Plugs, Cotton Wool, and Cloth Against a Workshop Noise Spectrum

The result. Against a 103.29 dBA workshop spectrum, well-fitted foam plugs give 72.74 dBA (dose 5.9 percent), poorly fitted foam plugs 94.33 dBA (863.3 percent), cotton wool 99.82 dBA (3071.5 percent), and a cloth wrap 100.11 dBA (3283.6 percent); the minimum effective attenuation required is 18.29 dB, or 21.29 dB with a 3 dB margin. Proven in simulation.

Two Wires Between Houses: Speech Reach, Ring Reach, and the Copper–Steel Sizing Rule for a Magneto Field Telephone

The result. Speech (1000 ohm loop budget) reaches 22.44 km on 1.0 mm copper, 5.89 km on 1.5 mm steel, and 23.56 km on 3.0 mm steel; ring reach is 5.0 times speech reach on every wire; wire costs USD 126.67 per km (1.0 mm copper) against USD 30.52 per km (1.5 mm steel), and steel wins on wire cost within its reach but cannot serve beyond 23.56 km in any gauge tabulated. Proven in simulation.

Sounding in Fog: The Frequency Band That Carries Across Water, and Why a High-Pitched Boat Whistle Does Not Reach a 600-Metre Crossing

The result. A 124 dB (at 1 m) horn carries 826.0 m in fog at 125 Hz and 739.1 m at 250 Hz but only 178.8 m at 4000 Hz; the band that carries across the stated 600 m crossing is roughly 125 to 500 Hz; bare minimum source 121.0 dB at 1 m at 125 Hz (0.426 m horn mouth), recommended specification a 0.90 m horn at 127.5 dB with a fog range of 1199.9 m; the commonly sold high-pitched boat whistle (4000 Hz, 115 dB at 1 m) reaches only 113.8 m in fog and falls 52.4 dB short of the criterion at the far bank — it does not work as a fog signal for this crossing. Proven in simulation.

The result. At 70 dBA a shout reaches only 1.259 m and at 80 dBA only 0.398 m; conversation at 1.0 m at 80 dBA requires 86.0 dBA, above a maximum sustained shout, with a daily vocal dose of 955.5 normal-voice hour equivalents; a $15.00 amplifier with 12 dB gain cuts the dose by a factor of 15.85. The Speaking Tube: Useful Length, Bend Allowance, and a Sizing Rule for a Two-Storey Workshop

The result. At normal speech a straight tube serves 21.6 m at 25 mm diameter and 86.3 m at 100 mm, with four bends reducing those reaches to 9.3 m and 37.0 m; on a 10 m, four-bend two-storey workshop run, 25 mm delivers 64.51 dB and does not meet the 65 dB criterion, while 38 mm delivers 66.73 dB and 50 mm delivers 67.76 dB — fit 50 mm, five bends or fewer. Proven in simulation.

The String Telephone, Priced Honestly: A Level Budget and the Distance at Which Speech Gives Out

The result. Fixed cup coupling losses consume 35.0 dB of the 55.0 dB budget (63.6 percent); the remaining 20.0 dB gives maximum intelligible distances at 10 N tension of 25.0 m on cotton twine, 40.0 m on nylon line, and 80.0 m on taut steel wire. The coupling losses, not the string, set the limit. Proven in simulation.

The Ultrasonic Pest Repeller, Tested by Arithmetic: A Disproof of the Whole-Room Claim

The result. Representative stated device (100 dB at 1 m, 40 kHz) reaches the generous 80 dB effect criterion only 5.12 m in a clear line of sight and 1.6 m behind furniture; furnished-room coverage under the stated shadowing model is 27.5 percent, and through a wall the level at 3 m is 62.7 dB, below the criterion — the whole-room and whole-house claims do not survive the arithmetic. Proven in simulation.

The Upwind Shadow: Where Wind Refraction Ends a Horn Signal, and Where to Place the Post

The result. Horn 112 dB at 1 m, free-field range 1.13 km; in a moderate 5 m/s daytime wind: upwind 115 m, crosswind 703 m, downwind 1450 m; dawn inversion restores crosswind to 1130 m but upwind only to 124 m while wind blows; calm dawn restores 1130 m in all directions. Placement rule: post upwind of the settlement so listeners are downwind. Proven in simulation.

The Grain Mill's Noise Budget: Daily Dose by Task Rotation, and What Distance, a Mud-Brick Enclosure, and a Shorter Rotation Each Buy

The result. Baseline rotation dose 2112.7 percent (time-weighted average 98.25 dBA); distance to 2 m gives 681.8 percent, a 12 dB mud-brick enclosure gives 326.9 percent, and the enclosure plus a rotation of 0.5 hours on each loud machine and 6.5 hours on quiet work gives 87.3 percent at 84.41 dBA; the enclosure costs $156.00 and requires 480 mud bricks. Proven in simulation.
