Fuel from the land

Cooking fuel grown and gathered: charcoal, briquettes, woodlots, dung and biogas — yields, regrowth bounds, and the claims that fail their own arithmetic.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114524

The Cold Bound on Household Biogas: Temperature, Heating Cost, and the Climates a Small Digester Cannot Serve Year-Round

The result. An unheated household digester with a 25 percent warm-temperature margin meets a 1.6 m3 per day cooking minimum only at digester temperatures of 31.78 degrees C or above, and self-heating holds that output only down to 26.2 degrees C ambient even when insulated.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114505

The Minimum Herd for a Cooking Household: A Collectable-Dung Bound and Digester Sizing Rule

The result. Cooking on biogas alone requires 8 cattle, 15 pigs or 198 poultry birds on collectable dung alone, and households below 5.714 kg of collectable cattle volatile solids per day cannot reach 1.6 m3 per day however large the digester.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114535

Dung Plus Kitchen Waste: A Co-Digestion Bound and a Bucket Rule for Households

The result. Kitchen waste is limited to 22.22 percent of daily volatile-solids loading by acidification rather than by the carbon-to-nitrogen ratio, giving a 13.5 percent gas gain at fixed loading and a household rule of one bucket of kitchen waste to five buckets of cattle dung.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114295

The Binder Bound for Residue Briquettes: Minimum Binder Fractions for Sawdust and Charcoal Fines and Their Cost in Heat

The result. Minimum binder fractions for a 0.30 MPa handling criterion are 5.0% starch, 7.5% molasses and 15.0% clay for sawdust and 6.0%, 10.0% and 20.0% for charcoal fines, with starch cheapest in heat on both feedstocks and clay on charcoal fines costing 6.00 MJ per kg (20.0% of its heat).

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114339

Charcoal Fines Recovery: Minimum Binder Fractions, Heat per Kilogram, and the Clay Limit for Briquettes

The result. Briquettes from charcoal fines pass a defined three-drop handling test at minimum binder fractions of 0.1591 for clay and 0.0921 for starch, giving 24.386 and 27.895 MJ/kg, and clay stops being worth its transport weight at a fraction of 0.3103, falling below dry wood itself at 0.3448.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114316

The True Wood Cost of One Kilogram of Charcoal: From Green Gathered Wood to Saleable Product

The result. Counted from green gathered wood at 0.45 moisture with bark, drying, and handling losses, one kilogram of charcoal sold costs 11.299 kg of green wood in an earth-mound kiln, 7.617 kg in a brick kiln, and 6.139 kg in a metal retort — 2.169 times the oven-dry figures for every method.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114376

The Coppice Rotation That Maximises Yield: A Cutting Rule, the Cost of Cutting Off Time, and What Stool Death Takes Away

The result. For the stated eucalypt coppice curve the yield-maximising rotation is 7.19 years at 15.07 cubic metres per hectare per year, cutting one year early or late costs only about one per cent, but unreplaced stool death at 5 per cent per cycle drops yield to 9.50 by the tenth cycle.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114495

Dung Cakes Priced Twice: Heat Delivered per Kilogram and the Fertiliser Destroyed by Burning It

The result. One kilogram of dried dung cake delivers 2.165 MJ of useful heat and destroys 15.05 g of nitrogen, and burning is rational only when the grain response to available nitrogen stays below 32.4 kg per kilogram and the fuel it replaces must be bought — it is not rational when the alternative fuel costs nothing.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114533

The Gathering Return Bound: Energy per Hour and the Distance at Which Gathering Stops Paying for Twigs, Leaf Litter, and Fallen Branches

The result. At 1 km, fallen branches return 75.97 MJ per hour of gathering labour, twigs 57.71 MJ per hour and leaf litter only 23.78 MJ per hour, and leaf litter stops paying beyond 4.87 km under a stated tenfold margin over metabolic cost, while twigs and branches are limited by the working day rather than by energy.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114290

Charcoal Kiln Yields Compared from Stated Assumptions: A Saleable-Yield Bound for Earth-Mound, Brick, and Metal Retort Kilns

The result. Counted the same way, saleable charcoal per kilogram of oven-dry wood is 0.192 kg for an earth-mound kiln, 0.2848 kg for a brick kiln, and 0.3534 kg for a metal retort, so a maker should order wood against the low yield — 6250.0, 4012.8, and 3162.6 kg respectively for a 1000 kg charcoal target.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114325

A Sizing Rule for a Hand Lever Press: Achievable Pressure, Briquette Density and the Usable Density Window

The result. A ratio-20 hand lever press at a sustainable 250 N delivers 1.503 MPa and places sawdust at 899.8, charcoal fines at 900.7 and rice husk at 605.7 kg per cubic metre, all inside the usable density window of 550 to 1050 kg per cubic metre (Proven in simulation).

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114346

Residue Briquettes Priced in Delivered Heat: Rice Husk, Coffee Husk and Groundnut Shell

The result. Groundnut shell (4.392 MJ delivered per kg, 0.046 kg ash per meal) and coffee husk (3.696 MJ per kg, 0.095 kg ash per meal) are workable briquetted cooking fuels, while rice husk (2.16 MJ per kg, 0.509 kg ash per meal) is not.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114518

How Much Crop Residue Can Leave the Field as Fuel? A Non-Additive Constraint Account for Maize Stover

The result. Combining the soil requirements by their maximum rather than adding them, 2.0 tonnes per hectare of maize stover (40% of production) can leave the field as fuel — 33,138.8 MJ per hectare, cooking for 3.026 household-years — with soil-carbon maintenance as the binding constraint, while the additive account wrongly returns a negative removable quantity.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114577

How Much Wood a Solar Cooker Actually Saves: The Displacement Bound Set by Meal Timing, Weather and Use

The result. With unchanged meal timing a solar cooker displaces only 0.09 of annual fuelwood under base assumptions and at most 0.30 even with perfect weather and use, so the claims that it halves fuel use or replaces firewood do not survive the arithmetic for that household pattern.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114559

Solar Cooking by Season and Latitude: Where and When a Small Box Cooker Actually Cooks

The result. In 8 of 16 latitude and season combinations the pot never reaches 100 degrees C, beans succeed in only 8 of 16 and flatbread in none, with a winter peak of only 19.8 degrees C at 45 degrees latitude.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114413

The Village Wood Balance: A Deficit Settlement, Its Growing Collection Radius, and the Year the Walk Reaches Forty-Five Minutes

The result. The stated village burns 1,168.0 tonnes per year against 260.0 tonnes of regrowth, a deficit of 908.0 tonnes per year that clears 60.53 hectares per year and takes the one-way gathering walk past forty-five minutes in 2065.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24114336

The Woodlot Regrowth Bound: How Much Land One Household Needs to Cook Forever

The result. One household needs from 1.123 hectares of arid woodlot on an open fire down to 0.034 hectares of humid woodlot on a high-efficiency stove to cook forever, with growth rate (a factor of 10.0) a stronger term than stove efficiency (a factor of 3.33).
