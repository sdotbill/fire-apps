Air, fire and disaster

Warning and refuge for the hours when things go wrong: radon and smoke witnessed cheaply, homes hardened against embers priced per measure, flood and earthquake warning bought for dollars, and kits for the first days after a disaster.

What Actually Saves the House: A Priced Cost-Effectiveness Ranking of Ember-Attack Home Hardening, and the Cheapest Bundle That Buys a Stated Survival Probability

The problem. Homes are lost to ember attack in wildfire country, and scarce hardening money is spent on the wrong measures.

The result. Vent mesh and a 0–1.5 m non-combustible wall zone are the highest-value ember-hardening measures, and the cheapest bundle reaching a modelled survival probability of 0.80 in a severe ember event costs US$6,100, while full-house window replacement is the poorest value and no spending on the six-measure menu reaches 0.90.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108450

A Passive Indoor Radon Witness Cheap Enough to Matter: Design and Fully Computed Sensitivity Budget for a 90-Day CR-39 Diffusion-Chamber Detector at Under US$5 in Parts

The problem. Indoor radon exposure in homes that cannot afford electronic monitors.

The result. A passive CR-39 radon witness with US$4.75 in parts, deployed for 90 days, computes to a practical detection limit of 8.56 Bq/m³ and 18.9% uncertainty at 100 Bq/m³ — enough to screen homes against both the WHO 100 Bq/m³ reference level and the EPA 148 Bq/m³ action level with error rates of a few percent, but not enough to certify a borderline concentration (Proven in simulation).

A Heat-Wave Survival Room: The Integrated Design, Computed Cooling Budget and Cost per Protected Person for One Cooled Room per Dwelling

The problem. Heat waves killing people indoors in dwellings with no cooling.

The result. Envelope treatment cuts the reference room's peak cooling load from 1,819.3 W to 801.5 W, and a $2,300 core package holds the room at 28 °C (wet-bulb 22.20 °C) through a 46 °C heat-wave day for $4.37 per protected person per heat-wave day, with a 5.0 kWh battery and 2.0 kWp of photovoltaics riding an 8-hour daytime outage.

Methane Leak Detection at Walking Pace with Phone-Grade Thermal Optics: An Honest Bound

The problem. Methane leaks that go unseen because survey cameras cost as much as a house.

The result. Even under optimistic assumptions, a walking survey with phone-grade thermal optics costing about US$300 cannot detect methane leaks smaller than 82 g/h at 1 m or 334 g/h at 10 m, so it can see only gross leaks.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24108595

Earthquake Early Warning from Cheap Accelerometers: An Honest Latency-and-Geometry Budget, a Blind Zone, and a Priced Verdict

The problem. Earthquake deaths in regions with no early-warning system because the instruments are priced for rich countries.

The result. A cheap dedicated-node network delivers 9.4 seconds of warning at 50 km and 23.5 seconds at 100 km for a magnitude 6.5 event, but inside a blind zone of about 15 km (25 km for phones) no warning is possible at any node density, and density beyond roughly one node per 100 km² buys almost no additional time.

A Flood Witness Network for the Settlements That Flood First: Design and a Computed Lead-Time Law for Low-Cost River and Rain Gauge Nodes at US$94 per River Node

The problem. Flash floods killing people in poor settlements that get no official warning.

The result. A network of US$94.00 river nodes spaced 5 km apart is computed to buy 75 minutes of warning on a 200 km² catchment at slope 0.003 at US$1.35 per warned household per year over five years, but buys no usable river-gauge warning on a 10 km² catchment, where only evacuation of people under a rain-based watch is served (Proven in simulation).

A Post-Disaster Drinking-Water Triage Kit for the First 72 Hours: Staged Treatment, Computed Log Removal, Gravity Throughput and a Priced Verdict

The problem. Disease from unsafe water in the first days after floods and earthquakes.

The result. A 1.70 kg, $58.00 three-stage gravity kit produces 56.36 L/day of safe water (0.972 L per dollar per day) on moderate 50 NTU water, but its safe output is zero on extreme 500 NTU water where chlorine demand exhausts the dose and virus removal falls to 2.0 log against a 4.0-log target.

A Lightning Witness for Schools and Clinics: A $91 Sferics and Electric-Field Warning Node, Its Computed Warning-Time Budget, and the Limits of the 30-30 Rule

The problem. Lightning deaths at schools, clinics, and sports fields in regions with no warning infrastructure, where the only guidance is the 30-30 rule, which cannot react until a nearby strike has already occurred.

The result. A $91 sferics-plus-electric-field warning node gives a school or clinic a median 29.2 minutes of warning before the first dangerous lightning strike — tens of minutes in most storms, but zero warning in 12.7% of them, a failure share a $318 three-node district arrangement cuts to 5.2% (all figures Proven in simulation).

A Post-Disaster Power Triage Kit for a Small Clinic: Load Definition, Photovoltaic and Battery Sizing, and Load-Shedding Rules

The problem. Clinics going dark after disasters — vaccines spoiled, births and surgery by flashlight.

The result. A 2.8 kW photovoltaic and 14.4 kWh battery kit costing USD 10,070 keeps a small clinic's refrigeration and medical devices powered through a seven-day storm week under explicit load-shedding rules, but empties on the ninth consecutive storm day unless a generator or cold-chain evacuation intervenes.

A Wildfire-Smoke Refuge Room for a Classroom or Clinic Ward: An Integrated Sizing Law and a Priced Comparison of Box-Fan Filter Arrays and HEPA Purifiers

The problem. Children and patients breathing wildfire smoke indoors for days with no refuge.

The result. For a typical 180 m³ room in mean wildfire smoke of 350 µg/m³, four box-fan MERV-13 units (sized for day-five filter loading, not day one) hold indoor PM2.5 at or below 35 µg/m³ for $0.514 per protected occupant per smoke day — while at peak smoke with no filter change only the HEPA class holds the target, and in leaky rooms neither class can, so sealing comes first.

A Heat Witness for Outdoor Workers: An Honest Error Bound for Cheap Heat-Stress Estimation

The problem. Heat stroke killing farm, construction, and delivery workers who have no heat-stress instrument.

The result. A US$38 on-site temperature/humidity sensor with a computed globe correction is the best cheap WBGT witness (RMSE 1.05 °C, required safety margin 2.0 °C for a 1% under-reading bound), free station data needs a 4.6 °C margin, and a wearable physiological estimator needs 8.6 °C and is not useful as a sole WBGT trigger — all Proven in simulation.

A Household Cookstove Smoke Witness and Chimney-Draft Design Bound: What a Sub-US$20 Carbon Monoxide Witness Can Resolve, and the Flue Dimensions That Keep a Cooking Fire's Smoke Out of the Room

The problem. Household air pollution from solid-fuel cooking — among the largest environmental killers of women and children.

The result. A sub-US$20 carbon monoxide witness can sound a reliable alarm at cooking-peak concentrations but cannot certify compliance with the WHO 24-hour guideline (its smallest resolvable exceedance is 2.3 times the guideline), while a 0.15 m diameter, 3.5 m flue costed at US$49.10 keeps a 3.0 kW cooking fire's smoke out of the room in calm air and a 5 m/s adverse wind.

A Disaster Mesh Communications Budget for a Town Without Grid or Towers: Coverage, Traffic, Power and Price for the First Week

The problem. Families and clinics cut off from all communication after disasters.

The result. A solar LoRa-class mesh of 8 relays and one handset per household can carry a 7500-household town's coded first-week traffic (11,796 messages per day) at 0.989 delivery and $42.91 per covered household, but shadowed districts need 24 m relays at the slowest rate and the single-channel backhaul collapses below 90% delivery above 29,057 messages per day.

A Flood Witness for Low-Water Road Crossings: Computed Warning Time, Sensor Placement and the False-Closure Trade-Off

The problem. Drivers drowned at flooded low-water crossings, the deadliest single setting in flash floods.

The result. A solar-powered crossing witness delivers 15.28 minutes of warning on a small steep catchment at a ten-year cost of US$2,472.08 per crossing, but only 1.67 minutes on the smallest steep catchments, where a physical barrier — not a beacon — is the answer.

A Corrected Low-Cost Indoor Particle Witness: Humidity, Coincidence and Scattering Corrections for Classification Against the WHO 24-Hour PM2.5 Guideline, Below US$60

The problem. Families breathing polluted indoor air with no trustworthy affordable way to know.

The result. Correcting a low-cost indoor particle counter for humidity, coincidence and scattering raises its simulated correct classification against the WHO 24-hour PM2.5 guideline from 81.8% to 89.7% across six non-fog home scenarios at a component total of US$55.50, but at fog-level humidity it classifies at only 49.8% and must not be trusted.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24109071

A Household Flood-Safe Store for Settlements That Flood Every Year: Raised-Fixed versus Sealed-Buoyant Design with Computed Elevation, Anchoring, Moisture and Cost Budgets

The problem. Families losing their stored food and seed to floods they know are coming.

The result. Against an expected annual loss of $301.28 for ground-level storage, a raised-fixed store at 1.68 m costs $22.34 per household per year and a sealed buoyant store anchored to 20.0 kN costs $44.10, and in Class IV floods both designs fail and only relocation serves.
