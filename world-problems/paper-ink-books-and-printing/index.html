Paper made, printed and bound in small workshops: inks, layouts, glue and wire, each priced per sheet and per book.

A Box That Buys Decades: Alkaline Reserve, Acid Migration and the Service Life of a Low-Cost Storage Box for Books and Papers

The result. A storage box board with a 3 percent alkaline reserve protects its contents for 12.0 years at 20 degrees Celsius and a high-buffer 8 percent board for 32.0 years, but the high-buffer board's life falls to 16.0 years in a 30-degree store, so reserve and cool storage together, not either alone, deliver decades of protection.

Thick Book, Heavy Bill: Paper Bulk, Grammage and the Postage Cost of a Three-Hundred-Page Book

The result. For a 300-page A5 book, a 70 gsm bulky paper gives a 446 g book with a 16.8 mm spine, against 493 g and 14.4 mm for 80 gsm standard paper; against a 90 gsm standard paper the bulky choice also saves USD 0.80 per posted copy by staying in the lower postal band while looking thicker, not thinner.

Four Pages at a Time: An Imposition Bound for A5 Booklets on A4 and A3 Sheets That Minimises Trim and Signature Waste

The result. An A5 booklet must contain a multiple of four pages: a 50-page text becomes a 52-page booklet with 2 blank pages (3.8 percent waste), a 32-page booklet needs exactly 8 A3 sheets, and at the stated sheet prices paper cost per booklet is identical on A3 and A4 stock, so the stock choice should be made on equipment and labour, not paper price.

Carbon Ink That Stays in the Bottle and on the Page: Pigment Loading, Opacity and Settling Time for a Low-Cost Writing and Printing Ink

The result. For a carbon ink whose opacity saturates with pigment loading, 6 grams per litre is the lowest loading meeting the stated document optical density of 1.2, with a settling life of 113 days and a pen-safe viscosity, whereas 20 grams per litre raises density only to 1.83 while cutting settling life to 28 days.

When Duplex Printing Saves Nothing: Reprint Losses, Jam Rates and the Net Paper Saving From Printing on Both Sides

The result. Duplex printing saves 49.8 percent of sheets with no user errors, 47.3 percent at a 5 percent reprint rate and 39.8 percent even at 20 percent errors; the saving is not exhausted until the reprint error fraction reaches 0.998, so duplex should be mandated for standard long documents and management effort spent on templates rather than on policing short jobs.

Envelopes From the Offcut: Blank Sizes, Sheet Nesting and the Envelope Format That a Small Cutter Should Standardise On

The result. Nesting rectangular envelope blanks on a 650x900 mm sheet gives 6 DL blanks per sheet at 24.5 percent waste, 4 C5 blanks at 36.0 percent waste, and 9 C6 blanks at 13.8 percent waste, so C6 is the format a small cutter should standardise on for this sheet size, with the other formats quoted at their true waste penalty.

Cutting the Ream Without Waste: A Guillotine Layout for Exercise Books That Recovers the Trim Strip as Usable Product

The result. On a 700x900 mm parent sheet, the stated 300x420 mm exercise-book blank fits 4 per sheet in portrait orientation with 20.0 percent trim, against 3 per sheet rotated; the leftover arrives as a single 100 mm side strip wide enough to cut into secondary products such as bookmarks and labels instead of being discarded.

Drying Without Warping: Airflow, Sheet Thickness and Time to Safe Moisture for Handmade Paper Dried on Racks

The result. A 2 mm handmade sheet dries to safe moisture in 15.6 hours in still air at 60 percent relative humidity, in 5.9 hours under a 1.0 m/s fan, and in 31.2 hours if formed 4 mm thick in still air; at 80 percent humidity the same 2 mm sheet needs 18.0 hours against 6.0 hours at 40 percent, so airflow and humidity each matter as much as thickness.

A Safe Humidity Band for Stored Paper: Curl, Jam Risk and the Cost of Running a Copier Outside It

The result. Jam rate stays at its baseline inside a 45–55 percent relative humidity band and rises to 4.7 jams per thousand sheets at both 30 and 70 percent humidity; at 20,000 sheets a month that raises monthly jam cost from USD 36.00 in band to USD 84.60, an extra USD 48.60 that placement and sealed wrappers, rather than room plant, can recover.

Ink by the Square Metre: A Coverage Rule That Predicts Ink Use per Thousand A4 Pages Within a Stated Dot-Gain Allowance

The result. At 20 percent nominal coverage on A4, ink use is 0.017 kg per thousand pages costing USD 0.30 at the stated ink price, rising to 0.076 kg and USD 1.37 per thousand at full coverage; one kilogram of ink therefore covers about 58,824 pages at text-like coverage, and including dot gain in the conversion is what makes the prediction usable for pricing.

Make-Ready Is a Fixed Tax: Combining Small Jobs on One Sheet and the Saving From Gang Printing Common Sizes

The result. Combining three representative short jobs (200, 300 and 500 sheets) into one gang run cuts their total cost from USD 49.62 to USD 40.54 at the stated rates, saving USD 9.08 (18.3 percent) and 36 make-ready sheets, because the duplicated setup and spoilage charges simply cease to exist.

Washing Recycled Pulp Until It Stops Paying: Deinking Yield, Brightness and the Optimum Number of Wash Cycles for Small-Scale Production

The result. For a 100 kg batch of recovered newsprint under the stated rates, two wash cycles are optimal: brightness reaches 58.0 points, yield is 95.6 kg, and net value is USD 38.43, compared with USD 21.00 unwashed and USD 25.93 after eight washes, because further washing spends fibre faster than it buys brightness.

Staples That Hold and Staples That Tear: Wire Length and Leg Formation for Saddle-Stitched Booklets by Page Count

The result. For the stated book paper, a 32-page booklet needs only a 6 mm staple leg, a 64-page booklet needs 8 mm, and 6 mm legs suffice up to 48 pages; longer legs loaded by default on thin booklets fold badly in the clinch and tear centre pages, so leg length should change at the tabulated page counts.

Choosing a Screen Mesh for Poster Printing: Ink Deposit, Edge Definition and Cost per Hundred Posters

The result. Ink cost per hundred posters at the stated size and coverage falls from USD 7.32 on a 43 threads-per-centimetre mesh to USD 3.08 on a 90 mesh, a saving of USD 4.24 per hundred, while the coarsest mesh that still holds a 0.30 mm line is 77 threads per centimetre — so mesh should be chosen by the finest detail in the job.

How Much Glue Is Enough: Spine Adhesive Thickness, Pull Strength and Cost for Paperback Books Bound in Small Workshops

The result. Page-pull strength saturates with adhesive thickness: the thinnest application clearing the stated 60 N school threshold is 0.3 mm, giving 70.7 N for USD 0.016 of adhesive, while a habitual 1.0 mm application gives 94.0 N for USD 0.053 — more than three times the adhesive cost for a third more strength.

The Run Length at Which a Stencil Duplicator Stops Beating a Photocopier: A Cost Crossover for Schools and Churches

The result. At the stated prices — a USD 0.35 master, USD 0.30 of setup labour, USD 0.008 per duplicator copy and USD 0.035 per photocopy — a stencil duplicator becomes cheaper than a photocopier at 25 copies; at 100 copies it costs USD 1.47 against USD 3.50, and at 300 copies it saves USD 7.40.

Refill Twice, Then Replace: Cartridge Yield Decay, Print Quality and the Economic Life of a Remanufactured Toner Cartridge

The result. Refilling a toner cartridge costs USD 0.63 per hundred good pages on the first refill against USD 2.14 for a new cartridge, rising to USD 0.92 at the third refill and USD 1.50 at the fifth, by which point spoilage has reached 31.9 percent of pages — so cartridges should be tallied at each refill and retired on spoilage in their middle cycles.
