Dangerous work

Falls, dust, noise, heat and heavy loads in the world’s informal workplaces — the exposures priced, and the protections that fit the arithmetic.

Capsize Budgets for Artisanal Fishing Boats: Hydrostatic Loading States and Low-Cost Stability Guidance for a 7 m Open Boat

The result. The first common loading state to go unstable is the flooded bilge/live-well free-surface state (GM −0.091 m from a 0.474 m free-surface correction), and a ~US$20 longitudinal subdivision recovers 0.380 m of that correction, returning it to GM 0.289 m. Proven in simulation.

A Stability Witness for Small Fishing Boats: Paint, a Plumb Line, and a Number the Crew Can Check Before Leaving the Beach

The result. A USD 16.00-per-boat witness set (load marks + two-person heel test + freeboard/bilge check) detects a 25% centred overload with probability 0.997 via the marks and 300 kg mis-stowed 0.6 m off centreline with false-pass rate 0.000 via the heel test — while the heel test alone cannot detect centred overload (false-pass 0.99–1.00), and 100 L of bilge water cuts effective GM from 1.382 m to 0.939 m. Proven in simulation.

The Borax Smelting Step for Mercury-Free Gold: An Honest Energy, Loss, Throughput and Grade Budget for Direct Smelting of Gravity Concentrate

The result. Borax smelting at 1,150 °C (20.50 MJ fuel, $5.80, 5 batches/day) recovers 94.5% at 2,000 g/t for $3.07/g with zero mercury versus amalgamation's 82.0% and 3.0 g Hg lost per batch, but it stops working below a hard concentrate-grade boundary of 714.3 g/t, which the gravity circuit must deliver.

Cutting Pesticide Exposure by Nozzle and Drift Physics: Droplet Spectra, Settling and Evaporation Trajectories, and a Costed Low-Drift Configuration

The result. Replacing a standard flat-fan nozzle (3 bar) with a designed low-exposure configuration (air-induction nozzle at 2 bar, 0.35 m height, shield) cuts off-target deposition 11.12-fold (31.48% → 2.83% of applied) and the operator airborne cloud 91.21-fold, at an efficacy cost of droplet density falling from 451.3 to 44.5 droplets/cm² and coverage from 31.9% to 19.5%, for US$352 incremental on a 12 m boom or US$19.00 on a knapsack — Proven in simulation, with far-field, vapour-drift, inversion, and near-field ground-fallout limits printed at full size.

Silica from Handheld Rock Drilling in Artisanal Mining: The Wet-Drilling Retrofit, Budgeted

The result. Wet drilling cuts the driller's silica TWA from 4.29 to 0.296 mg/m³ at the surface (4.59 to 0.312 mg/m³ underground) for an $87 kit, but still leaves the driller at 5.9–6.2 times the 0.050 mg/m³ limit, forcing a fit-tested P100 respirator as an additional layer.

Lead Exposure Control for Informal Battery and E-Waste Recycling: A Computed Baseline and a Priced Process Change Set

The result. Full wet-breaking + LEV + separation + housekeeping set cuts worker lead exposure by 96.0% for $2,750 capex, but residual TWA is still 61.0 µg/m³ (above the 50 µg/m³ OSHA limit) and a child beside one-year legacy soil still receives 406.4 µg/day — respirators, child exclusion, and legacy-soil action are also required. Proven in simulation.

Collective Fall Protection for Informal Construction: A Low-Cost Perimeter Guardrail Sized from Site Materials

The result. Sized for a 1.54 kN stumble load, timber, bamboo and braced scaffold-tube guardrails pass at $6.49–$13.26 per metre ($273–$557 per typical building) while rebar fails as a rail at every practical spacing, and the guardrail covers an estimated 65.6% of worker-hours at height — leading-edge erection work is not covered.

A Blowout Witness for Fresh-Concrete Formwork on Informal Construction Sites

The result. The plywood panel governs formwork capacity at 40.5 kPa — below an ordinary 2.0 m/h, 20 °C pour (48.7 kPa) — and an USD 8.00 reference-line deflection witness warns at 30.6 kPa (24.4% margin; 12.4 min at 2.0 m/h) with an 18.54% false-alarm rate and 0.17% missed-detection rate; it is defeated by self-compacting concrete, joint/prop failures away from midspan, and fast pours that consume the time margin. Proven in simulation.

Mercury-Free Gravity Gold Recovery for a Village Circuit, Priced Against Whole-Ore Amalgamation: A Designed Circuit with a Recovery Budget

The result. A sluice + shaking-table gravity circuit recovers 53.8% of the gold at $5.05/g (64.4% at $6.41/g with a small centrifuge) versus 31.9% at $8.71/g for whole-ore amalgamation, which also loses 7.978 kg of mercury per year — while the gravity route recovers only 16.6% of the gold finer than 38 µm. Proven in simulation.

Heat and the Piece Rate: Work-Rest Design for Harvest Labour That Does Not Quietly Tax the Worker's Pay

The result. On a hot day peaking at 32.0 °C WBGT, the safe work–rest schedule cuts a conventional-shift cane cutter's piece-rate earnings by 71.9% ($72.00 to $20.25); a split shift plus shade-and-water stations plus paid rest makes the worker whole for $7.78 extra per tonne, and above 30.0 °C WBGT (27.5 °C unacclimatised) no schedule protects and work must stop. Proven in simulation.

Heat Limits and Work-Rest Design for Brick-Kiln Labour

The result. At midday the allowable work fraction is zero for every kiln task (stop thresholds 30.0–31.1 °C WBGT vs 34.8–37.1 °C in the yard zones); safe work exists only at dawn (carrying 32.2 min/h, stacking 40.4 min/h), and the compliant rescheduled day costs 73.2% of carrying output, raising cost per 1,000 bricks carried from $5.00 to $18.62.

Knapsack Sprayer Operator Exposure: A Computed Exposure Budget and a Low-Cost Protective Set Built Around Closed Transfer

The result. An unprotected knapsack operator absorbs 28.215 mg in a typical day (470.3% of a reference AOEL, 68.0% of it from open mixing), and a $27.00 protective set built around closed transfer, a seal kit, hose rerouting, gloves, and a light overshirt cuts that to 1.181 mg nominal (19.7% of the AOEL), while an impermeable coverall stores 100 W of heat, fails within the hour in tropical conditions, and protects less well than the light set once wearing compliance is counted.

Power Take-Off Shaft Guarding: The Entanglement Budget in Time and Torque, and a Guard Specification That Follows From It

The result. At 540 rpm a loose sleeve is fully taken up in 0.121 s (inside a 0.20 s best-case response) and a 75 kW PTO exerts 60,286 N at the shaft surface (75x a braced two-arm pull), so only a barrier protects; the derived guard spec plus friction-reducing features cut the modelled seasonal guard time penalty from 273.5 to 74.0 minutes.

Silica Dust Control for Village-Scale Stone Crushing: A Computed Exposure Assessment and Priced Control Design

The result. Water suppression (36.0 L/tonne), partial enclosure, and operator repositioning cut the computed silica exposure from 821.0 to 42.9 µg/m³ — below the OSHA limit of 50 but above the ACGIH limit of 25, and above the OSHA limit in four computed failure cases, so respirators remain a required layer.

Thresher Feed Guarding: Keeping the Drum Beyond Reach — A Hazard Budget and Feed-Chute Design

The result. A 1.10 m feed chute at 35 degrees with a 0.45 m by 0.14 m aperture keeps the thresher drum 0.226 m beyond the longest modelled arm reach, at a computed throughput cost of 11.8 percent — necessary because a grabbed hand crosses a typical 0.30 m unguarded gap in 0.012–0.15 s, far inside reaction time. Proven in simulation.
