Boiling Water Cannot Make Low-Acid Food Safe in a Jar: Spore Inactivation Times at the Boiling Point, and the Altitude at Which Even Acid Processing Times Break

The result. At 100 °C a boiling-water bath needs 5.41 hours to deliver 12 decimal reductions of Clostridium botulinum spores, rising to 7.129 days at the 85 °C boiling point of 4502 m, while a stated 35-minute acid-food schedule already becomes insufficient at 396 m.

The Coldest Point in a Real Pot: How Long After the Surface Simmers the Middle of a Thick Stew Is Safe

The result. The centre of an unstirred 8-litre thick stew reaches 75 °C 24.2 minutes after its surface does, a lag that stirring cuts to 4.6 minutes.

How Long Cooked Food Can Sit: A Holding Bound from the Cooling Curve and a Growth Account

The result. No pot cooling at 20, 28, or 35 °C ever reaches 5 °C; the shortest Bacillus cereus standing limit is 8.01 hours (uncovered rice at 35 °C), giving a safety-factored rule of at most 5.0 hours at 20 °C and 4.0 hours at 28 °C or 35 °C, and reheating leaves the heat-stable toxin index unchanged at 16.2 two hours past that limit.

How Long a Cure Takes to Reach the Middle: Salt Diffusion Times by Thickness, Computed from Fick's Law

The result. A surface cure protects the centre in time only up to 5.97 cm thick at 4 °C and 4.15 cm at 12 °C; a 10 cm piece needs 39.33 days at 4 °C for its centre to reach 3.5 percent water-phase salt.

Keeping Dried Food Dry in a Wet House: Moisture Uptake Through Stated Containers, and the Barrier a Ninety-Day Store Requires

The result. A 2.0 kg dried pack can absorb only 75.1 g of water before passing the safe limit, and a container for a 90-day store in a 90% relative-humidity room must transmit no more than 8.16 g per m² per day — testable by weighing at an average gain of at most 0.834 g per day.

The Moisture Endpoint That Is Actually Safe: Water-Activity Targets for Dried Fruit, Fish, and Meat, Computed from Sorption Isotherms

The result. In hot-humid air at 75 percent relative humidity, open sun drying cannot reach the mould-safe water activity of 0.60 for fruit, fish or meat, because the equilibrium moisture lies above the target in every case.

How Much Salt a Vegetable Ferment Needs: A Lower Bound from Acidity, and an Upper Bound from the Fermenting Organisms Themselves

The result. The safe band is 2.0 to 3.5 percent brine (1.04 to 1.82 percent salt in the aqueous phase): at 5.0 percent brine the safety margin is negative (−0.12 days), so more salt is not always safer.
