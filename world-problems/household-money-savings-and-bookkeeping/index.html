Which Categories Belong in Cash? A Selection Rule for Envelope Categories Based on Overspend and Transaction Counts

The result. Pricing stated card-overspend factors against cash's hassle and forgone cashback, cash envelopes pay for online impulse purchases (net USD 563.33 a year), dining (USD 711.75) and entertainment (USD 277.20), but lose money on transport extras (USD -32.67); the selected set is worth USD 2093.68 a year.

Cashback Against Interest: The Payoff Fraction Below Which a Rewards Card Costs More Than It Returns

The result. Simulating USD 1,400 a month on a 1.5% cashback card at 24.9% APR, the break-even payoff fraction is 59%: at 90% payoff the household carries a steady USD 159.15 balance and loses money net, and one month's spending carried for three months costs USD 88.97 of interest, erasing 4.2 months of cashback.

The Coin Jar Examined: What a Year of Hoarded Change Costs Against Even a Modest Deposit Rate

The result. A USD 1.85-a-day change stream kept in a jar at home holds USD 5376.49 after ten years against USD 8291.54 if swept monthly into a 4.0% account — a gap of USD 2915.05, or 43.1% of contributions, with the three-year gap at USD 364.34 confirmed by stochastic simulation (median USD 500.92).

How Many Months Is Enough? Emergency Fund Size Against the Duration of Income Interruptions

The result. Against a stated interruption process (4% monthly onset, 30% income continuation, essential spending USD 2600), a three-month emergency fund is exhausted in 21.8% of interruptions; 7 months brings exhaustion under 5% and 7 months under 1%, with a 99.2% chance of at least one interruption per decade.

Setting the Cash Envelope: A Quantile Rule for Weekly Discretionary Caps That Bounds Overspend Without Starving Ordinary Weeks

The result. Setting a weekly discretionary envelope at the 90th percentile of desired spending (USD 250.99 for a distribution with mean USD 180 and standard deviation USD 55) limits breaches to about one week in ten, with a mean excess of USD 32.31 at the nearest tabulated cap and monthly exhaustion of a USD 150 buffer in 1.2% of months, whereas a cap at the mean is exceeded in 50.4% of weeks.

Subscription Creep Priced: How Much a Statement Audit Recovers, and How Often It Must Run to Be Worth Its Time

The result. Simulating an eleven-subscription household with a 3.5% monthly forgetting rate at USD 13.40 a forgotten subscription, never auditing costs USD 10069.21 a year in payments for unused services; a statement audit every six months gives the best total — USD 299.24 residual waste plus USD 24.00 of time, USD 323.24 in all.

Sizing the Irregular-Bills Fund: A Percentile Rule for Monthly Set-Asides Against Lumpy Annual Costs

The result. Simulating nine irregular bills a year (mean annual total USD 3228.30), the usual mean-divided-by-twelve set-aside (USD 269.03) still leaves 80.8% of years with a shortfall month; setting aside the 90th percentile instead (USD 378.96) cuts that to 55.0%, and a one-time USD 500 float at the mean-based amount cuts it to 52.4%.

A Set-Aside Percentage for Irregular Income: Keeping the Quarterly Tax Bill From Becoming a Debt

The result. Simulating irregular freelance income (median USD 4,100 a month) with a mean effective tax rate of 18.8%, setting aside 20% of each receipt still leaves an unpayable tax quarter in 61.1% of years; a 28% set-aside cuts that to 12.4% with mean shortfall depth USD 447.48.

How Often Should a Household Reconcile Its Books? Error Accumulation and the Optimal Interval Between Checks

The result. Simulating a ledger with 1.14 recording errors a month and harm accruing the longer an error survives, total annual cost (harm plus checking time at USD 7.50 a session) is minimised by monthly reconciliation at USD 101.47, against USD 52.17 quarterly and USD 68.00 for checking once a year.

Paying the Minimum, Priced: Clearing Time and Total Interest on a Card Balance Paid at the Minimum Versus a Fixed Sum

The result. A USD 3200 balance at 24.9% APR paid at the minimum (interest plus 1% of balance) takes 192 months and USD 5507.32 of interest to clear; fixing the payment at the first month's minimum of USD 98.40 clears it in 55 months, and a fixed USD 150 a month in 29 months at USD 1070.04 interest.

Living One Month Ahead: The Overdraft Protection Purchased by Holding Last Month's Income as This Month's Budget

The result. For a household earning USD 3,900 and spending USD 3,780 a month with early-month bill clustering, no buffer means 0.33 overdraft events and USD 10.12 of fees a year; living one month ahead cuts that to 0.00 events and USD 0.00, and the buffer takes 12 months to build at 8% of income.

The result. The result. The result. The result. The Arithmetic of the Small Daily Leak: What a Five-Dollar Habit Costs at Ten, Twenty and Thirty Years

The result. A five-dollar daily leak is 3.5% of a USD 52,000 income and compounds at 5.0% to USD 23632.02 in ten years and USD 126659.36 in thirty (USD 125108.37 for ten dollars a day over twenty years), reaching USD 10,000 in 4.9 years invested against 5.5 years uninvested.

Budget Billing for Utilities: What the Levelled Payment Buys, and What It Costs, Against Smoothing the Bills Yourself

The result. Simulating seasonal utility bills (mean USD 118.02, standard deviation USD 47.96), a household smoothing pot levels outlay completely and holds its own float (mean USD -32.18, negative in 58.5% of months), while utility budget billing levels outlay nearly as well (SD USD 4.37) but holds the float itself and, at a USD 2 monthly fee, costs USD 600 over twenty-five years.
