Moving Goods

Carts, boats, bicycles, pack animals and human backs: what it costs to move food and goods where there are no trucks, and the load each method can honestly carry.

Paddle or Carry: The Current and Length at Which Portaging a Rapid Beats Paddling Through It

The result. Portaging beats paddling above 1.60 m/s current (energy) / 1.65 m/s (time) at 100 W; paddling is impossible at or above 1.84 m/s, and an 800 m rapid in a current above 1.24 m/s fits neither method within a 600 kJ budget.

How Big Must a Wheelbarrow Wheel Be? A Bekker Sinkage Bound for a 100 kg Load on Sand and Soft Clay

The result. The common 0.30 m wheel needs 394.3 N on sand and 185.4 N even wide on soft clay against a 150 N sustained push limit; minimum diameter is 0.40 m wide on clay, while on sand no practical diameter suffices (0.94 m wide extrapolated).

A Bound on Head Portage: The Maximum Sustainable Daily Load–Distance Product for Head-Supported Loads

The result. The maximum sustainable daily load–distance product for a 60 kg head porter is 1.59 tonne-km/day at a 51.6 kg load over 30.77 km, while common 30–40 kg practice delivers only 0.81–1.14 tonne-km/day (51–72% of the bound).

Stability Bounds for a Two-Wheel Bicycle Cargo Trailer: Hitch Load, Roll, and Cornering Speed on Dirt

The result. Stable towing requires the load centre within a ~10 cm window just ahead of the axle (hitch fraction 0.07–0.15; mid-bed loading gives 0.25–0.49); on dirt the trailer slides before it rolls below 0.70 m combined centre-of-mass height, giving safe speeds of 14.6 km/h in a 5 m turn and 20.6 km/h in a 10 m turn, and a maximum load of 99.5 kg set by the trailer wheel ratings.

A Gravity Ropeway for Hillside Produce: Sizing, Throughput, and the Drop Below Which Gravity Drive Fails

The result. Baseline 400 m span / 60 m drop delivers 2,464 kg/hour with only 63 N net driving force; gravity drive requires a drop of at least 0.117 times the span, and the 12 mm rope safety factor of 2.21 is too low for a real build.

The Market-Day Weighing Queue: Why More Weigh Stations Cannot Fix a Compressed Arrival Pulse, and the Staggered Schedule That Can

The result. Under a two-hour arrival pulse of 240 farmers at 4.0-minute service, keeping the 95th-percentile wait under 30 minutes takes 7 weigh stations — beyond the 4 stations a $15,000 budget buys (95th-percentile wait 118.62 minutes, $1,373.64 expected spoilage per day) — while staggering the same farmers into eight hourly slots meets the bound with 3 stations (26.30 minutes, $251.67 per day).

How Long Raw Milk Survives the Bicycle Ride: A Q10 Acidity Bound and an Ice-Jacket Can Sized to Beat It

The result. Uninsulated raw milk reaches the 0.20 % acidity rejection threshold in 3.65 hours at 35 °C — less than the 6.25-hour worst realistic bicycle chain — while an insulated 10-litre can with 3 kg of ice keeps it below threshold for over 24 hours.

Rolling a Drum vs Carrying Water: Pull Force, Gradient, Step, and Time Bounds for a 90 Litre Drum on Dirt Paths

The result. A 90 L drum stays within a sustained 100 N pull only up to a 5.42% gradient on firm dirt and 0.40% on ordinary soft dirt, a 50 mm step needs 721.0 N, yet it fetches 90 L in 16.2 minutes against 73.7 minutes for five 20 L carrying trips.

A Safe-Load Bound for Cargo Behind the Rear Axle of a Small Motorcycle on Unpaved Roads

The result. The safe cargo load 0.60 m behind the rear axle is 42.8 kg, set by the front wheel retaining 25% of total weight for steering — the rear tyre rating alone would permit 71.0 kg; commonly observed 100–150 kg loads put the front wheel at 10.7–2.2% of total weight and the rear tyre at 120.0–154.5% of its rating.

The Shoulder Carrying Pole as a Spring: A Stiffness Window and a Sizing Rule for Two 20 kg Baskets

The result. For 2 × 20 kg the pole helps only inside a narrow stiffness window of 2,180–2,850 N/m (best pole, 1.9 m × 29 mm bamboo, cuts peak shoulder force only 5.1%, from 529.7 N to 502.6 N), while a resonant pole at 5,700 N/m raises the peak to 1,092.7 N, 106.3% above a rigid carry.

How Many Passengers Can Crowd to One Side? A Stability Bound for a Small River Ferry

The result. Maximum 23 passengers with stated cargo keeps crowded heel below 10 degrees (20 passengers gives 8.20 degrees with margin); a commonly observed full loading of 30 passengers heels to 13.15 degrees under a 70 percent crowd.

The 50 kg Grain Bag on Trial: A Spinal Compression Bound for Full-Day Portering and the Price of the Bag That Passes

The result. A solo 50 kg grain-bag lift produces 4,891 N of L5/S1 spinal compression, 43.9% above the 3.4 kN criterion, so it fails on the first lift of the day; the largest bag passing a full 12-tonne day is 21.55 kg (the priced fix, 20 kg bags, costs $154.75 per day or $12.90 per tonne), and 50 kg remains acceptable only team-lifted (2,466 N per porter in close geometry) or mechanically handled.

The Humidity Ceiling of an Evaporative Cart Cooler: A Psychrometric Bound for Market Produce at 35 °C

The result. At 35 °C and effectiveness 0.75 the cooler holds a 25 °C tomato threshold only below 28.2 % relative humidity and a 20 °C leafy-greens threshold only below 8.6 % — a disproof, in plain terms, of the claim that evaporative cooling protects produce on hot, humid days; where it does work it needs only 0.72 kg of water evaporated per market day.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113322

Startability of a Loaded Bicycle: A Gearing Sizing Rule from Pedal Force at the Worst Crank Angle

The result. At 150 kg total mass the lowest gear must be no higher than 22.7 gear-inches to start on a 6% grade and 14.3 on 10% (10.7 at 200 kg on 10%); a common 66 gear-inch single-speed cannot start the load above 1.53% grade at 150 kg or 0.95% at 200 kg, despite starting both easily on the flat.

Two Wheels or One: The Load-and-Grade Crossover Between a Two-Wheel Hand Cart and a Single-Wheel Wheelbarrow

The result. The wheelbarrow's hand load (26.7% of total weight) caps its sustainable payload at 52.5 kg on any grade, while the balanced two-wheel cart (7% tongue load) stays within stated human limits in 29 of 42 load-and-grade cells against the barrow's 6, with 13 cells feasible for neither.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113346

A Sustained Draft Bound for Donkey and Ox Carts: Working-Day Budgets, Grade Limits, and the Overload Line

The result. A commonly loaded 300 kg cart requires 176.6 N on level dirt, exactly a 180 kg donkey's entire sustained draft, so it overloads the donkey on any grade above 0.0% and needs 412.0 N at 8%; the donkey delivers 1.50 tonne-km on a cool level day against the ox's 5.87.

How Fast Is Too Fast for Tomatoes: A Vibration Damage Bound for Crates on Unpaved Roads

The result. An uncushioned crate of tomatoes on corrugated dirt exceeds the damage ceiling (damage index 1.0, defined as 10 % bruised fruit) above 20.3 km/h — reaching 2.185 at 30 km/h — while a 15 mm foam liner raises the limit to 37.0 km/h.
