Light After Dark

Light after sunset where the grid does not reach: lanterns, kerosene, charging kiosks and stored daylight — cost per useful lumen-hour, glare and flicker included.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113040

Flicker in Cheap LED Lanterns: A Perception and Headache-Risk Bound, the Cost of the Fix, and a Phone-Camera Certification Rule

The result. Two of five driver classes fail a stated low-risk flicker region, by factors of 11.88 and 5.31; the fix costs $0.30-$0.80 per unit and a phone camera detects the failures as 3.3 and 6.6 bands per frame.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113064

Sizing a Community Charging Kiosk: Ports, Panel, and Battery for a 200-Household Village, With the Cloudy-Week Failure

The result. 24 ports cut mean wait to 2.25 min versus 44.76 min at 20 ports, with a 500 W panel and 2789.6 Wh bank at $2.29 per household per year; a five-day cloudy spell accrues a 6333.5 Wh deficit the bank cannot cover.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113085

Light for Night Work: Task Illuminance Bounds and a Shared Work-Bench Design That Bounds Added Hours Without Inventing Productivity

The result. A $59 shared bench light delivers 584.4 lux at centre and bounds 3.5 usable added hours per evening (875 h per seat per year), while failing tailoring dark cloth at its 750 lux upper bound and falling to about 104.6 lux at the bench ends.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113112

Storing Light: A Levelised Cost per Useful Lumen-Hour Comparison of Batteries, a Flywheel, and Thermal Storage

The result. LiFePO4 stores light at $0.0252 per 1,000 lumen-hours over ten years; a flywheel retains only 26.21% after a 6-hour hold and thermal-TEG round-trip efficiency is 4.17% — both killed for diurnal lighting.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113133

Glare from Bare Cheap LEDs: A Discomfort-Glare Bound for a Small Room and the Cheapest Geometry That Meets It

The result. Bare 800-lumen LED lantern in a small room gives UGR 41.8 against a bound of 19.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113160

Task Light for Night Clinical Work: Required Illuminance, Colour Rendering, and the Cheapest Headlamp That Meets Both

The result. Suturing needs 1,000 lux and CRI>=90; a 120/320 lm CRI 92 headlamp with 3 cells (32.08 Wh usable, 16.9 h mixed) meets all tasks for $26.50, $34.50 as a kit with spares.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113162

Shared Lighting for a Night Market: One Central Solar Tower Against Distributed Lanterns, Priced per Useful Lumen-Hour

The result. Central 20,000-lumen solar tower never beats distributed 400-lumen lanterns on cost per useful lumen-hour ($12.73 floor vs $11.67), becomes cheaper per stall only above 55 stalls, but its edge falls below 10 lux beyond 50 stalls, and it leaves the whole market dark an expected 6 nights per year..

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113166

The Human-Powered Light Bound: How Much Light Ten Minutes of Effort Buys, and Where Muscle Beats the Sun

The result. Ten minutes of untrained pedalling (50 W) produces 658.1 lumen-hours — a full 1,000 lumen-hour night in 15.2 minutes — but solar beats muscle on cost wherever the sun shines; human power wins in emergencies and as a reserve.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113196

The True Cost of Kerosene Light: Cost per Lumen-Hour, Cost per Useful Lumen-Hour at the Desk, and an Evening Exposure Bound

The result. Simple wick kerosene costs $3.2828 per 1,000 useful lumen-hours at $1/L vs $0.0333 for a directed LED (98.58x), with a modelled 1,509.2 ug/m3 PM2.5 evening average at 1 ACH, 100.6x the WHO 24-hour guideline.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113218

Path Lighting for Safety: A Spacing Bound from Detection Distance, Its Cost per Kilometre, and the Fog Regimes Where No Affordable Spacing Works

The result. 600-lumen solar path light at 3 m: detection of an obstacle at 10 m requires 2 lux at the midpoint, giving 15.5 m maximum spacing at $3,559 per km per year.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113220

Daylight Amplification by Room Surfaces: How Much Light a Lime-Washed Ceiling and Walls Add, What It Costs per Lumen-Hour, and How Fast Kerosene Soot Takes It Away

The result. Lime-washing walls and ceiling raises mean workplane daylight by 83.75% fresh and 48.44% time-averaged on a 6-month kerosene-cycle recoat, at $0.00468 per 1,000 added lumen-hours, but kerosene soot halves the gain in 3.73 months.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113236

Daylighting Without Windows: The Plastic-Bottle Skylight and the Light Pipe, Their Delivered Lumens and the Bound Where They Fail

The result. 12 bottles hold 100 lux for 7.57 h on a clear day and 0 h overcast; one 350 mm pipe gives 246 lux clear noon, 31 lux dull overcast; both give 0 lumens at night.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113260

Sizing a Solar Lantern: Panel Watts and Battery Watt-Hours for 100 Lumens for Four Hours a Night, by Climate Class, with the Cloudy-Day Failure Line

The result. 100 lm for 4 h/night needs 3.899 Wh/night, a 2.0/2.5/4.5 Wp panel by climate class and 12.18 Wh (2-day) battery; a common 1 Wp/3.7 Wh lantern supports only 0.55 of a night per day in the humid class, failure at about 4 cloudy days.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113264

A Light Library: Lending Charged Lanterns Like Books — Fleet Size, Fee, and the Loss Rate at Which the Model Dies

The result. A 30-lantern library serves 160 households episodically at $4.01 per household per year, but dies at a loss rate of 1.063% of loans and fails outright for nightly demand; the poorest fifth is served free for $128.16 per year in total subsidy.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113285

Phosphorescent Path and Step Markers as a No-Power Light Source: A Charge-and-Decay Calculation and a Kill, With One Narrow Survival

The result. Kill: no phosphor class survives a winter night or is above 3 mcd/m2 at winter 2 a.m.; only premium strontium aluminate after a summer charge (12.38 h) survives a summer night.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113305

Light for Study: A Minimum Useful Illuminance Bound at the Page, the Cheapest Optical Design That Meets It, and What Illuminance Does Not Fix

The result. Acuity alone bounds study light at only 17 lux for 10-pt print; reserve-based bands are 150 lux minimum and 300 lux comfortable, met by a 1 W LED + reflector delivering 596 lux average over 0.12 m2 for $5.90 in components.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24113343

The Improved Kerosene Lamp, Tested to Destruction: Even the Best Case Loses on Cost and on Dose

The result. Best-case pressurised mantle lamp: $0.162 per 1,000 lumen-hours (14.5x the $0.011 LED+solar lantern) and a modelled 1,050 ug/m3 PM2.5, 70.0x the WHO guideline — killed for lighting on cost and dose together.
