Safe Bathing Enclosures for Open Coasts: Structural Loads, Current Exchange and Capacity Budgets for a Netted Swimming Enclosure

The result. A 100 m × 50 m netted enclosure holds 350 bathers comfortably, needs anchors rated to the fouled wave-added load of 180.0 kN (36.0 kN holding per anchor), flushes on a governing slack-water bound of about 9.3 h, and costs $0.61–$2.15 per protected bather-day from $175,250 capital — Proven in simulation.

A Sponson Retrofit for Small Open Fishing Boats: Righting-Moment and Capsize Budgets from a Parametric Stability Model

The result. In the combined worst loading case the unmodified 7.0 m boat has effectively no stability reserve (maximum righting arm 0.005 m, vanishing at 21.6°), while the recommended 0.50 m × 0.40 m × 3.20 m sponsons per side restore a positive range to 51.0° with a maximum righting arm of 0.311 m and cost approximately no speed in normal trim because they ride clear of the water at rest — all computed, Proven in simulation.

Cold-Water Immersion Timelines for Fishing Crews: A Heat-Loss Bound and the Protection Budget of Low-Cost Immersion Layers

The result. At 10 °C an immersed wearer in ordinary work clothes has a computed 24.7 minutes before incapacitation, rising to 36.8 minutes with a low-cost flotation layer, 80.8 minutes with a 3 mm neoprene layer, and 175.6 minutes in a full immersion suit — while the first-3-minutes cold-shock drowning risk is removed by no insulation in the set and is mitigated only by flotation.

The Loaded-Deck Bound: How Catch Stowed on Deck Consumes the Stability of Small Fishing Boats, and a Loading Rule Crews Can Apply Without Instruments

The result. In the stated hydrostatic model, catch carried on deck breaches pre-stated stability criteria at 244 kg on the representative 7 m boat (against 1,399 kg for the same catch stowed low), yielding a field rule — Proven in simulation — that deck catch must not exceed 10 percent of the boat's unloaded displacement, a bound that holds with a 22.5–71.8 percent margin across boats of 6–9 m.

Desalination for Boats and Small Islands, Priced Honestly: A Break-Even Bound Against Trucked and Shipped Water

The result. Boat watermakers cost $11.14–$15.31/m3 and lose on price to $3.00/m3 dockside water in every case computed, while well-used island RO plants at $2.08–$3.19/m3 beat shipped water at all distances but lose on short routes when utilisation falls to 20%.

A Coastal Erosion Witness Network for Data-Poor Shorelines: Survey-Post and Photogrammetry Accuracy Budgets and Trend-Detection Times

The result. In the stated model, a monthly tape-and-post or smartphone-photogrammetry witness network confirms a 0.5 m/yr shoreline retreat in 3.17 years at $655–$850.50 per kilometre per year, but any periodic scheme can miss an entire 10 m storm step for up to a full observation interval and up to 6.0 m of it can be masked by recovery on an annual schedule.

Ice-Free Fish Preservation at Sea: An Insulated Hold with Phase-Change Panels, Priced in Days of Catch Saved

The result. An 80 kg shore-charged phase-change panel set in a well-insulated 120 kg hold extends a tropical catch's saleable life from 0.45 days to 6.58 days at $1.47 per additional saleable day, but it cannot beat ice priced below about $0.09/kg.

Cutting Landing-Site Fish Spoilage Without Ice: A Solar-Assisted Dryer and Raised-Rack Design Priced per Kilogram of Catch Saved

The result. In simulation (Proven in simulation), a $608 raised-rack solar-assisted dryer for 100 kg/day split to 10 mm raises the season saved fraction from 0.75 (open-ground) to 1.00 — an additional 3,000 kg per 120-day season at $0.051 per additional kilogram saved — but it wins only at 15 mm thickness or thinner in sun and 10 mm or thinner on cloudy days, and at 20 mm neither method saves the batch on any day tested.

Buoyancy Budgets for Inherent-Flotation Work Clothing: A Design Study for Fishing and Waterside Workers

The result. A 2.895 kg inherent-flotation work clothing system provides 13.81 kgf net buoyancy at immersion in seawater decaying to a permanent 8.18 kgf, keeps a clear airway for tens of minutes for typical wearers with 70% of buoyancy on the chest, but fails to guarantee airway clearance for heavy lean wearers in freshwater and does not address cold shock — all proven in simulation.

A Harbour Wave and Surge Witness for Small Ports: Pressure-Sensor Detection Budgets, Seiche Warning and Moored-Vessel Alert Lead Times

The problem. Small harbours and marinas have no wave or storm-surge instrumentation, so moored vessels and harbour works are damaged without warning and harbour seiches part mooring lines unrecorded.

The result. A two-sensor bottom-pressure witness detects surge to 7.96 mm (drift-limited), gives 255.7 s of usable seiche warning before the modelled 30 kN mooring-load limit and 285.0 s of surge-front warning from a 3,000 m stand-off sensor, at an optimal deployment depth of 8 m and a capital cost of $3,550 per harbour. Proven in simulation.

Island Water and Power Sizing: A Rainwater-Catchment and Diesel-Hybrid Reliability Model for a Small Island Community

The result. For an 800-person island community, no storage tank can compensate for an undersized catchment — only above the 22,902 m2 mean-balance area does storage help, with 30,000 m2 plus 4,000 m3 (62 days) giving 96.2% monthly reliability at USD 1,582 per household per year.

Paper · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24109835

Wearability Budgets for Working Lifejackets: Thermal, Mass and Mobility Costs as Design Constraints on Devices People Will Actually Wear

The problem. Fishers refusing to wear lifejackets during deck work because of heat, bulk and interference with work, leaving them without flotation in the occupation with one of the highest drowning burdens.

The result. In the stated heat-balance model, an offshore foam vest cuts the estimated safe continuous work time in Warm conditions (28 °C) at heavy work from 241 minutes to 68 minutes — driven by a 27.3% rise in evaporative resistance and 32% body coverage rather than by its 0.95 kg mass, which costs only 3.2 W — and the inverted design envelope gives a zero insulation budget in Tropical conditions, where exposure management replaces device design as the binding constraint.

Mangrove Belts as Coastal Defence, Priced per Metre: Wave Attenuation, Growth Lag and Lifetime Cost Against a Seawall Baseline

The result. A target-meeting mangrove belt is cheaper per metre than a seawall ($1,018 vs $3,855 NPV for 213 m medium width over 50 years) but loses 7.08 years to growth lag, a 150 m medium belt fails its wave target at about a 1-in-4-year storm, and surge is essentially unattenuated.

Getting Back Aboard Alone: A Self-Recovery Aid Design Study for Single-Handed Small-Boat Fishers, with Grip, Strength and Freeboard Budgets

The result. A leg-driven ladder with a submerged stirrup and a load-bearing waist tether makes self-reboarding force-feasible in simulation for the weakest stated user at 0.60 m freeboard (where an arm-only climb fails by 402.8 N), with a design bound of 1.15 m maximum freeboard and a simulated reach-and-deploy probability of 0.519 at 0.5 m/s boat drift, at a three-station cost of £445.50 — Proven in simulation.

Mooring Loads Under Storm Surge for Small Craft: A Design Bound for Harbour Moorings When the Water Rises

The result. The representative berth's surge limit is 1.90 m, set by the pontoon lifting off its piles while the mooring lines are still unloaded — the lines alone would not reach 50% of breaking load until 3.54 m (3.44 m at 25 m/s wind), and only matched pile-and-line upgrades raise the limit, to at most 4.90 m for $8,008 per berth. Proven in simulation.

A Low-Cost Rip-Current Witness for Unguarded Beaches: Drifter-Release Detection Statistics and Warning Budgets

The result. In simulation (Proven in simulation), releasing 20 reusable GPS drifters every 10 minutes detects 79.6% of rip episodes with a mean 36.6-minute warning lead at a cost of $6,869 per beach for a first 90-day season, while at a 30-minute interval detection falls to 46.6%.

Soft Coastal Defences Priced per Metre: Wave Attenuation and Lifetime Cost of Brushwood, Geotextile and Dune Systems Against a Rock Revetment Baseline

The result. Brushwood, dune and geotextile systems cut reference erosion by 79–96% at $26.50–$38.00 per metre per year against $61.80 for rock, absorb 0.45–0.90 m of sea-level rise where rock absorbs none, but fail at incident wave heights of 3.0–3.8 m where rock survives to 4.5 m.
