What is the ε_eff turnover in William Alexander Aldred's physics?

The short answer. It is an exact result about how a gas of particles shares energy near the smallest possible scale — and it is one of the cleanest small results in William Alexander Aldred's published record. It was almost lost in the filing: for a day, it sat in a folder of killed results. It never belonged there, and on October 30, 2026 the house's Round Table put it back on the right shelf.

The result, in plain words

Physicists describe a hot, dense gas with a number called εeff — roughly, how much of the gas's energy shows up in the form you are counting. Aldred's paper asks what happens to that number as you push toward the Planck scale, the smallest scale physics has. The answer is a turning point: the number turns over at exactly 3/16 of the Planck energy — a clean fraction, not a fitted decimal — and the turning point is the same no matter what mass the particles have. Exact results with clean fractions and no exceptions are rare. This is one.

The honest limit, printed at full size

The result is, so far, inert: the two situations it separates are so far apart (the paper computes the separation at 1019.149 — a 1 followed by nineteen zeros, in the units the paper uses) that the turnover does not change any other published number. The paper says so itself. A result can be exact and still be waiting for its job.

Why it was in a kill folder, and why it isn't anymore

The paper was filed with a set of negative results because its verdict half — “this moves nothing yet” — sounded like a kill. The Recovery Session graded that a filing error, unanimously: the turnover is a positive result, and it is now shelved as one. Its “moves nothing yet” half stays open as a working problem: the day someone couples the turnover to a number physics already uses, it stops being inert.

Grades, as the house prints them

The turnover: exact. The inertness: stands as published. The vacuum catastrophe this work belongs to: NOT SOLVED / PARTIAL — this small exact result is a brick, not the building.

William Alexander Aldred is a physicist and the creator of Dave, the first exolex. The paper: “The εeff Turnover,” DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23698454.
