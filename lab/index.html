What you're seeing

A universe collapsing under its own gravity, watched by its density. Time runs left to right and the bounce sits at the centre. The strong gold curve is the density, drawn as a fraction of the Aldred Limit (ρA = 2.92×10⁵⁵ g/cm³). The pale curve is the size of space itself — the scale factor — shrinking, bottoming out, and growing again.

Why it behaves that way

Gravity alone drives density to infinity: the singularity. Here the density carries its own brake. The equations gain a term that grows with the square of the density, and at ρA it cancels the pull exactly — collapse halts at the cap and runs back out. Past half the cap (the dotted line) the effective pressure has already flipped sign: that is the null energy condition reversing, and it is what slows the fall before the stop.

Now drag the torsion slider. Torsion is what geometry does with the spin of matter (the Riemann–Cartan extension of Einstein's theory). Spin pushes back, harder the denser things get — so the bounce fires earlier and softer, and the peak density drops well below the cap. Same universe, two different rescues.

The engineering principle

Every real material has a limit load: steel yields, a pressure vessel has a rated maximum, and a competent engineer designs with the limit inside the equations, not pasted on afterwards. The Aldred Limit says spacetime has a rated maximum too — a greatest possible density, written into the geometry itself.

Units: time is measured in bounce units τ (1 τ ≈ 2.5×10⁻²⁵ s); the content is radiation, so ρ ∝ A⁻⁴.

What you're seeing

Two coils — one in the pad, one in the vehicle — each tuned to ring at the same frequency, like two identical tuning forks. Energy crosses the gap only while the two stay in step. The curve is the delivered efficiency at every separation; the gold dot is where your pad sits right now.

Why it behaves that way

The strength of the handshake is the coupling, k. Separate the coils and k collapses — a loop's magnetic field falls off roughly as the cube of the distance — and the efficiency falls off the cliff you see in the curve. Q is how many swings a coil rings before its energy leaks away as heat: high Q gives the energy many chances to hop across instead of leaking. Detuning is the two forks at slightly different pitches — energy sloshes across and sloshes back before the receiver can keep it. Set the detuning to +3% and the phase-match to zero, and watch the pad bleed.

Then raise the phase-match. That control is the fifth-coordinate element of this design: a tracker that continuously re-times the drive so the two resonators stay in step. It cannot fix distance — nothing can — but it rescues a mistuned pad almost whole.

The engineering principle

Wireless power is a conversation, not a broadcast. The whole game is the figure of merit U = k·√(Q₁Q₂): coupling times quality. Everything else in a real product — alignment guides, ferrite shaping, phase tracking — exists to protect U.

The model: standard coupled-mode theory, identical coils, no fitted material data — the honest first pass an engineer runs before reaching for a field solver.
