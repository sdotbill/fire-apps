Now my worked example — this class's set problem, worked in full. The problem: Take the paper “The Acoustic Cut-off Frequency of the Solar Atmosphere from Scale Height and Sound Speed, and the Reflection of Five-Minute Oscillations”. Its stated result is: The paper computes its solar quantity from published observations and compares it with the torsion bounce frame's fixed scales (bound density 9.72e+54 g/cm3; solar central density at a fraction 1.54e-53 of that bound). (a) Restate the claim in your own words. (b) Name one observation or computation that could prove it wrong. The worked answer: A strong answer restates the claim without borrowing the paper’s wording, then names a test the paper itself would accept — here, the natural test is to recompute or re-measure the headline quantity in the stated result and check it against the paper’s own number, under the paper’s own stated assumptions.
