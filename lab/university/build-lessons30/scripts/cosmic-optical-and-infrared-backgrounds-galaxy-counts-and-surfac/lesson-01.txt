Now my worked example — this class's set problem, worked in full. The problem: Take the paper “A Baryon Budget for the Near-Infrared Excess: What a First-Stars Explanation Would Cost”. Its stated result is: Computed budget study (2601 words). Headline computed values: I for fstar 0.001 nW = 0.2866; I for fstar 0.01 nW = 2.866; f star needed for 12nW = 0.04188; f star needed frac of baryons pct = 4.188; observed stellar baryon frac today pct = 6. (a) Restate the claim in your own words. (b) Name one observation or computation that could prove it wrong. The worked answer: A strong answer restates the claim without borrowing the paper’s wording, then names a test the paper itself would accept — here, the natural test is to recompute or re-measure the headline quantity in the stated result and check it against the paper’s own number, under the paper’s own stated assumptions.
