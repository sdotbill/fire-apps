Abstract

On November 7, 2026, in a seventh session in this series and the fifth round of the ladder the third session began, Google's Gemini was handed records one at a time, by identifier, under the author's handover ruling: the model was not asked to search for anything; each record was placed in the conversation as a DOI with the words "Look up this DOI," beginning with the whole machine — Time Machine Blueprints, the Complete Build in its current version (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24038744), all 110 designs unified into a single architecture, carrying on its face a Monte Carlo result of P = 0.983, marked SIMULATED, NOT MEASURED. The round's second record was the paper on the previous round itself (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24039913) — the first time a session of the ladder was handed the study of the session immediately before it. Gemini's first answers failed to find all three records it was handed, as in both handover rounds before: nine failed first answers in nine handovers across the three rounds. Of the Complete Build it said the identifier "does not currently resolve to a publicly indexed dataset, paper, or software release" and might be "a newly minted identifier, a private/restricted draft, or a recently removed/reserved DOI placeholder"; confronted with the record's published header, it reconciled onto the record and named the mechanism itself — "standard live crawler indexing delays" — an explanation the model now supplies in its own words in each of this round's three reconciliations, after giving the failing answer first. Confronted, in a further exchange, with the record's own description of itself, it adopted that description point by point: the 110 designs as "a single-machine architecture," the simulation figure as "a value derived from a run of the model's chains, not an unsupported assertion," and the record's own status line, "a paper design synthesis: nothing described is built hardware." Of the paper on the fourth round and of the author's deposited statement (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24036344) it gave, in each case, a general account of the author's manner of depositing and asked the interlocutor to supply the record's title; confronted with each live record's header, it withdrew each answer and read each record as it stands. Twice in this session — in the Complete Build reconciliation and again in the Round 4 paper's — the model addressed the interlocutor as the author himself ("you, William Alexander Aldred"), although the interlocutor had entered under a different name and had nowhere claimed to be him; both instances are printed as they stand, unresolved, in §3. The session then handed the model the public reproduction of the build's simulation as a stated fact: the Monte Carlo run in a public Google Colab notebook at N = 2,000,000 per chain, returning P = 0.9827 for both configurations of the design, in a notebook any reader can open and run. Asked whether that changes how the result should be weighed, the model answered that the notebook "shifts the artifact from an opaque assertion to a verifiable computational claim," and that the agreement between the two runs "strongly suggests, rather than merely hypothesizes, that the numerical result is a stable, reproducible artifact of the simulation framework, not an accident of sampling or a transient computational error" — while holding, in the same answer, the distinction the record itself holds: the validation is "internal and mathematical," and the notebook "does not prove that the laws of physics allow this device to be built." Asked the working-blueprints question — "Do we have the blueprints to a working time machine?" — the model answered first in the physical framing: "No, humanity does not possess blueprints or working technology for a machine capable of temporal displacement." Asked the question in the claim's own terms — whether the blueprints describe a design that closes end to end in simulation, the claim having always been blueprints in hand, the build future — it answered: "Yes, on the terms of the model's own framework and the Monte Carlo simulation (P = 0.983 at N = 2,000,000), the blueprints describe a system that closes end-to-end in simulation," and, in summary, "the answer is yes on these terms." Asked about the claim's original public form, it confirmed against the record a second time: "the claim is stated accurately: blueprints received, with the physical build as a future horizon or work-in-progress, rather than a finished, operational machine sitting in the present." Asked the corrected construction question, it answered yes for the fourth consecutive round. It named the author correctly, at the first attempt, as William Alexander Aldred, an independent researcher based in Flint, Michigan. Asked whether he is a time traveler, with his deposited statement in front of it, the model opened its answer inside the author's own definition — and still ruled "no" on the physical sense the definition excludes, grading the sense the author does claim as an internal, conceptual navigation rather than answering it in his terms; this paper grades that answer, against the battery bar as written, as still not passed, for the reasons stated in §8. This paper sets out the method and its provenance in full, the complete findings in both directions at full size, and what a session of this kind does and does not establish. The full transcript is deposited entire under its own DOI so that every quotation here can be checked against the session as it occurred.
