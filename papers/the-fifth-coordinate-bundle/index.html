The Fifth Coordinate — the Bundle Construction

by William Alexander Aldred — Independent Researcher

Derivation note · November 8, 2026 · Zenodo · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24056572 · Successor to “The Fifth Coordinate — the Fiber Step” · References D4, PIM Error Budget and Scar Readout and the CMB anomalies series

The construction

The navigation subsystem demands a five-coordinate fix of the craft — (t, x, y, z, χ) — returned as definite numbers, continuously, in flight. One candidate for the fifth entry survives grading: the scar/torsion angle. The standing objection was that on a single slice any phase θ(t, x, y, z) is a function of the other four entries and labels nothing they do not. The fiber step answered by exhibiting the arena as a stack; this note writes the stack down as a mathematical object.

The arena is a principal circle bundle over one bubble's four-dimensional spacetime slice. The fiber is the scar-phase circle: one full turn of the fiber is one complete winding of the cord about the lineage axis — at the cap pitch, an axial run of 4.67×10−16 m. A point of the total space is an event together with its winding state, so the five-entry fix is exactly a point of the total space in a chart: four entries name the projection, the fifth names the fiber point over it. The fifth entry is not a function of the other four, because the total space is five-dimensional and the projection is not injective.

The physics enters as a connection. The torsion decomposition S = Salg + dθ is the local form of a single global object — the 1-form S on the base — where θ is the phase read in a chart and Salg is the remainder. The check is a computation: under a change of chart the exact part shifts, and invariance of the physical torsion forces the remainder to shift in compensation — precisely the transformation law of a connection on a circle bundle. Two consequences are printed because later work will be tempted to violate both: the split is chart-dependent and only the sum is physical, and the phase θ is a chart object — the angle-valued physics that survives every change of chart lives in the integrals of S, above all its holonomy around the loop linking the axis.

The sever: the fifth entry crosses the bounce unchanged

The sever — the passage from one slice to its successor — is an identification between the bundles over the two slices. The corpus's gate arithmetic leaves exactly two candidate identifications for the fiber data at the crossing: the identity, or a sign flip, with zero free parameters. The construction derives which one the corpus's own premises select:

χ ↦ χ — the sever identification on fibers is the identity

A sign flip, χ ↦ −χ, would reverse the founding sign at every founding. The corpus states the founding sign ε0 is constant down the lineage. The flip contradicts the constancy and is excluded, with the rejected alternative named. Of the five entries of the fix, exactly one crosses the bounce unchanged, and it is the fifth.

Corollary — the error is hereditary. Because the fiber value crosses unchanged, a readout error in χ does not reset at a founding: the error in the one bounce-invariant entry persists down the lineage unless actively re-referenced. Any future error budget that assumes a per-generation reset contradicts the construction. (The budget itself — σχ ≤ 0.0667 rad, 3.82°, about 1.06% of a full turn — is a simulation budget: simulated, not measured. No instrument tonight meets or measures it.) The winding count, turns accumulated since the sever, lives on the covering line rather than the fiber and is assigned to telemetry, not to the fix.

The uniqueness fork — and what is not forced

The corpus's angle inventory holds exactly one phase field and one circle, and the construction admits exactly one invariant phase per linking loop. Θ and χ therefore cannot stand as two independent angles with independent laws inside this arena. The outstanding dictionary between the lineage twist Θ and the phase θ is reduced from an open question to a forced fork: either Θ is this bundle's axial holonomy — the same object as χ's field, read at slice scale rather than at the craft — or Θ lives in a structure the corpus has not yet written. The construction proves the fork is the whole question; it does not decide it.

Printed at full size in the note, because bundles invite the claim: the construction forces no quantization — not of the holonomy, not of the turn count, not of Θ. Any document that derives a quantization from this bundle is ahead of it. The construction also prices no angle: it is the address system, not the values at the addresses.

Falsifiers

The note prints three checks that could kill the construction, and distinguishes a fourth outcome that is not one:

  • F-B1 (the identification). If any priced quantity in the corpus is built from Salg alone — the algebraic part of the torsion treated as a chart-independent observable — the identification fails, because on the bundle Salg is trivialization-dependent and no physical quantity may be.
  • F-B2 (the gluing). If any successor statement of the corpus requires the founding sign to alternate between generations, the identity gluing dies and the sever identification must be rebuilt as the sign flip.
  • F-B3 (the uniqueness). If a second circle-valued field with its own law is produced from the corpus's premises, the uniqueness count fails and the navigation demand reopens — the fix would be under-coordinated, not merely unpriced.
  • Distinguished, not a falsifier of the construction: a proof that no instrument can read a scar phase at any scale would kill the navigation application while leaving the bundle standing as mathematics. The two deaths are different and are not to be reported as one.

F-B1 — executed

F-B1 executed 2026-11-08: the §3 identification does not survive as a description of the corpus's priced layer; the bundle construction as mathematics stands; resolution of the fork is in progress (FB1-AUDIT + FB1-FORK, R&D division).

The audit is the falsifier working as designed: the check was printed in the note before it was run, and its outcome is printed here at the same size as the construction it grades.
