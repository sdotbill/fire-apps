The Equatorial Witness: the founding-profile reading of the Cold Spot fails its registered falsifier

by William Alexander Aldred — Independent Researcher

Result note · November 8, 2026 · Zenodo · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24053823 · Result for Paper 2, “The Cold Spot at 123.4 Degrees”

The result

A prediction was written down before the data were read, and the sky has now graded it. The founding-profile reading of the CMB Cold Spot — the branch of the bounce framework's account that survived Paper 2's internal grading — fails its pre-registered falsifier, F-CS1. The branch is dead, at full size, finally: two independent executions of the measurement on the Planck PR3 sky agree on every graded number, and the verification record is deposited with the result.

This page is the public face of that result. Nothing on it is softened, and nothing beyond the graded record is claimed.

What was predicted

Paper 2 of the CMB anomalies series, “The Cold Spot at 123.4 Degrees,” computed two readings of the Cold Spot. The standard reading assigns the spot to the Eridanus supervoid; the paper showed the void's expected decrement is ≈ −20 μK against the spot's ≈ −150 μK at center — present, aligned, and insufficient at standard amplitude. The competing reading places the spot inside the founding profile of the bounce framework: a ring system about the alignment axis at galactic (−100°, 60°), of which the spot, at polar angle 123.40°, would be the most visible arc.

That reading survived Paper 2 on exactly one branch — temperature proportional to the negative of the profile (cold = −profile) at the single amplitude ratio A3/A2 = +2.1406 — and the branch made a complete set of predictions for the azimuthal average ⟨ΔT⟩(θ′) about the axis: a deepest cold extremum at the axis itself, a hot ring at 68.70°, a cold ring at the Cold Spot's latitude 123.40° at 0.264 of the axis depth, a hot cap at the anti-axis, and zero crossings at 43.53°, 98.67°, and 148.70°.

Its sharpest prediction is the equatorial witness. At θ′ = 90° the octopole vanishes identically, so the profile's value there carries no amplitude freedom at all: on this branch, the azimuthal average 90° from the axis must be hot, at +0.1592 of the axis cold depth. Paper 2's falsifier F-CS1 registered the consequence in advance: if the measured average shows no such structure, the surviving branch fails — and the witness alone, if cold, executes it at one angle.

What was measured

The measurement was pre-registered — statistics, tolerances, map, and pass/fail lines fixed in writing before any Planck pixel was read — and executed on the Planck PR3 SMICA temperature map with the PR3 common intensity mask (77.6% of sky, Nside 512, monopole and dipole fitted on good pixels only). The equatorial witness band, 89–91° from the axis, is:

⟨ΔT⟩(89–91°) = −6.02 μK — cold

Predicted hot, at W/Dpt = +0.1592. Measured W/Dpt = −0.2774 (W/Dcap = −0.2437). The value is identical computed from the ring table and recomputed directly from the band's 32,400 raw pixels. Under the registered rule, the branch dies at the equator on this number alone.

The two passes are printed as measured facts, not as partial support: F-CS1 is conjunctive, and clause (i) is decisive by itself. A rotated-axis null — the full statistic chain recomputed about 1,000 random axes on the same masked map — returns praw = 0.7280 and pjoint = 0.2110: the witness value is not special on this sky by this statistic. The branch fails not because the equator is anomalously cold, but because the template required it to be hot and it is not.

Verification, on the record: the first execution's own diagnostic pass found two defects on its face — a sanity check written for a raw-sky product and an ungraded sensitivity variant — and the kill was held provisional until both were resolved. The corrections were itemized in a dated patch list before the rerun; no statistic, tolerance, or pass/fail line was changed. The verification rerun passed its geometry assertion (axis-to-Cold-Spot angle 123.399°, against the papers' 123.40°), its input-dipole audit (+0.3 μK), and graded the inpainting-mask variant (100.0% of the primary good set retained; witness identical). The verdict is final.

What the verdict covers — and what it does not

What died: one template branch — the azimuthal imprint of the founding profile read as cold = −profile at A3/A2 = +2.1406 — as the explanation of the CMB Cold Spot. Together with Paper 2's confinement argument against the opposite sign branch, the founding-profile account of the Cold Spot at multipoles {2, 3} is closed. No wider profile is fitted in its place: with unpriced coefficients at multipoles 4 and 5, a ring could be placed at the spot's latitude by construction, and this program does not fit.

What was not graded: falsifier F-CS2 (whether the line-of-sight density and lensing fields produce the full −150 μK at standard ISW amplitude — a measurement on those fields, not on the CMB map) and F-CS3 (the sign of the transfer under Paper 1's propagation law). Their absence from this verdict is on the record.

What is not executed: the bounce/torsion framework itself, the founding-axis results of Paper 1, and the remaining faces graded in Paper 3. This test addressed one branch of one paper's reading of one feature.

What stands untouched: Paper 2's constraint on the standard reading. The Eridanus supervoid supplies ≈ −20 μK against the spot's ≈ −150 μK central decrement — a minority contribution at standard amplitude. This measurement does not repair that shortfall; it removes the competing founding-profile account at {2, 3} and leaves the spot's depth, on present evidence, under-explained by both readings.
