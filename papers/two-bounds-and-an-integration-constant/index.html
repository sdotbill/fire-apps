Two Bounds and an Integration Constant: The Kopczyński–Trautman pin, the de Sitter floor, and the open value problem

by William Alexander Aldred — Independent Researcher

Published October 20, 2026 · Zenodo · 15 pp · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23485358

Abstract

Einstein–Cartan (EC) gravity contains a spin-sourced correction to the effective energy density that can halt collapse at a finite density, and a positive cosmological constant imposes a late-time floor on the expansion. This note places those two bounds in a single framework alongside a Henneaux–Teitelboim unimodular sector transplanted into EC, in which the cosmological constant is demoted to an integration constant and, at the classical level, constant vacuum shifts decouple from the traceless field equations. This note does not solve the cosmological constant problem.

For the radiative-instability tier it claims only that decoupling, within this framework and at that level; it organizes the value tier into a three-link chain in which one link is a known result inside a labeled model, one is an identification by construction, and one is open with no calculation yet in existence; and it makes no claim on the coincidence tier. The ultraviolet bound discussed here is the Kopczyński–Trautman bound of 1972–73, not a new result; its sub-Planckian pin is a property of the dust spin-fluid reading used in that literature, and under the self-consistent relativistic ε(n) evaluation the balance retreats to ∼the Planck density. The infrared floor is standard de Sitter asymptotics. No derivation of the floor's value from the bounce state currently exists, and this note makes no empirical prediction distinct from ΛCDM plus the cited EC literature. A numerical near-equality sometimes presented as a two-clock coincidence is shown to be bookkeeping — the same combination of constants up to an exact factor of 8π/3. What remains is a research program with stated kill conditions, not a result.
