Annual Modulation of Dark-Matter Recoil Rates: Amplitude, Phase and the Recoil Energy at Which the Sign Reverses

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24151794 This study computes the annual modulation of nuclear recoil rates caused by the motion of the Earth around the Sun, for candidates screened by a mass-dependent density bound. The modulation fraction rises from a few per cent at low recoil energy to more than ten per cent near the kinematic endpoint, and its sign reverses at low energy for light candidates because the velocity integral samples a different part of the halo distribution in June and in December. The computed peak date stays close to the start of June for the standard halo, and moving the halo circular speed by 20 kilometres per second moves the reversal energy measurably while leaving the peak date almost unchanged. A modulation claim therefore carries three testable numbers, amplitude, phase and reversal energy, and a signal that matches only the phase has passed the weakest of the three. All results are computed, not measured.

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24151831 This study computes the flux of dark matter particles boosted to relativistic or semi-relativistic speeds by collisions with cosmic rays, and the recoil signals such particles would produce in large underground detectors. Boosting solves the threshold problem for light candidates: a MeV-scale particle that would leave an undetectable recoil at halo speeds can leave a kiloelectronvolt recoil after a cosmic-ray collision. The price is flux, which falls with the same scattering cross section that produces the detectable recoil, so the signal scales as the square of the cross section and the reachable region in parameter space is a band rather than a half-plane. Large neutrino detectors, not dedicated dark matter detectors, set the relevant exposures. All results are computed, not measured.
