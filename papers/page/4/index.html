Gravitational-Wave Standard Siren Distances in a Bounced Cosmology: From GW170817 to Third-Generation Reach

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24150125 Gravitational-wave standard sirens measure luminosity distance from the waveform alone, and their sensitivity to a bounced cosmology grows steeply with the redshift their detectors reach. The nearby event GW170817, at about 44 Mpc, has its distance interpretation shifted by -0.009 percent at epsilon = 0.10, far below its 15 percent distance uncertainty. At redshift 0.5, representative of second-generation bright sirens, the shift is -0.51 percent; at redshift 1.0, -1.01 percent; and at redshift 2.0, within third-generation reach, -1.72 percent. Since the expansion rate inferred from a siren at fixed redshift scales inversely with its distance, these are also the fractional biases an uncorrected standard-frame analysis would carry. A catalogue of sirens at redshift near one with percent distances would bound the bounce amplitude near epsilon = 0.100, making sirens, within a decade, competitive with the acoustic scale as frame witnesses while sharing none of its physics. This study is computed from stated assumptions, not measured.

Strong-Lens Time-Delay Distances in a Bounced Cosmology: The Distance Combination and Its Sensitivity

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24150149 Strong gravitational lens time delays measure a combination of angular diameter distances to the lens, to the source, and between them, and that combination samples intermediate redshifts where a bounce correction is partly switched on. For a representative lens at redshift 0.5 with a source at redshift 2.0, the time-delay distance changes by 0.20 percent at epsilon = 0.10; across a grid of lens and source redshifts it ranges from 0.11 to 0.24 percent. Published time-delay analyses quote per-lens precisions near 5 percent and combined expansion-rate uncertainties near 2 percent, centred in the low seventies. Matching the computed shift to the combined precision gives a tolerance amplitude of epsilon = 5.350. Time-delay cosmography is therefore moderately frame-sensitive: it cannot be ported into a bounced cosmology at face value, but recomputed it becomes an independent intermediate-redshift witness with leverage the local rungs lack. Tully-Fisher Distances in a Bounced Cosmology: Rotation-Based Moduli and the Peculiar Velocity Field

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24150177 The Tully-Fisher relation turns a spiral galaxy's rotation speed into a luminosity and hence a distance, and it is the workhorse of peculiar-velocity cosmology. Recomputing Tully-Fisher distances in a bounced cosmology separates two effects that are often conflated. The distances themselves, calibrated locally and applied within about 100 Mpc, shift by 0.0002 mag at 50 Mpc and 0.0005 mag at 100 Mpc for epsilon = 0.10, against a per-galaxy modulus scatter near 0.4 mag. The inferred peculiar velocities, however, are differences between observed redshifts and distance-predicted redshifts, and at 100 Mpc the frame changes the predicted recession velocity by 1.6 km/s, comparable to the small-scale velocity noise but systematic in character, growing linearly with distance. Bulk-flow measurements that reach beyond 100 Mpc must therefore either adopt a frame or bound this term; within the classic Tully-Fisher range the effect on published bulk flows is below 0.4 percent. Type Ia Supernova Distance Moduli in a Bounced Cosmology: Residuals Across Redshift and the Amplitude They Permit

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24150181 Type Ia supernovae are the first distance-ladder rung that genuinely samples the redshifts where a bounce correction switches on, and their moduli bound the correction directly. Relative to the standard frame, the bounce frame at asymptotic amplitude epsilon = 0.10 changes distance moduli by -0.0021 mag at redshift 0.1, -0.0112 mag at redshift 0.5, -0.0221 mag at redshift 1.0, and -0.0376 mag at redshift 2.0. Supernovae alone therefore permit only a modest early-time modification, and they locate its observable signature at redshift above 0.5, where the coming generation of surveys is most sensitive.
