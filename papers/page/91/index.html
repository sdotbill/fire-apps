Conductive Evaporation of the Local Interstellar Cloud at the Hot Bubble Interface

William Alexander Aldred · November 13, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24193844 A warm cloud embedded in million-degree gas is heated at its surface by electron conduction, and the heated layer flows away: the cloud evaporates. Applying the classical conduction-front mass-loss formula to a cloud of radius two parsecs facing the Local Bubble interior gives a mass-loss rate of 8.8e-07 solar masses per year and an evaporation time of only 0.4 million years for a cloud of 0.35 solar masses. That time is far shorter than the age of the bubble, so classical unmagnetised conduction would have destroyed the local clouds long ago. Their observed survival is therefore quantitative evidence that conduction at the interface is suppressed, by the factor of at least a few tens that a magnetic field draped over the cloud surface provides. The discrepancy is the result: it measures the suppression the interface must have. The rate is computed from stated published inputs with the deposited script, not measured.

William Alexander Aldred · November 13, 2026

Adopting the published integrated value of 0.90 electronvolts per cubic centimetre, the cosmic-ray pressure is P/k = 3,481 K per cubic centimetre, about 1.66 times the cloud thermal pressure and about a third of the hot bubble interior pressure. Cosmic rays are therefore the largest single support term inside the cloud, and their rough equality with the magnetic pressure is the well-known coincidence now confirmed in the Sun's own neighbourhood. In the piled-up plasma of the hydrogen wall, where the charge-exchange cross-section at thermal energies is much larger than at kiloelectronvolt energies, the mean free path falls to about 22 astronomical units, giving an optical depth of 0.90 across a twenty-astronomical-unit wall layer and a naive transmission of 0.41. Detailed models return a milder filtration near one half, because atoms exchanged in velocity space partly refill the beam, and the gap between the naive and modelled values measures the importance of that refilling. How Many Supernovae Does the Local Bubble Energy Budget Require?

William Alexander Aldred · November 13, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24194017 The Local Bubble holds thermal energy in its hot interior and kinetic energy in its expanding shell, and both reservoirs were supplied, on the standard picture, by supernovae in the Scorpius-Centaurus association. This study adds the two reservoirs from published size and pressure values and divides by the useful energy delivered per supernova. The interior thermal energy is 1.14e+51 erg and the shell kinetic energy is 2.91e+50 erg, so at a coupling efficiency of ten percent the budget requires about 11 supernovae, comfortably inside the fourteen to twenty explosions reconstructed from the association's stellar history. The agreement is a consistency result rather than a proof, because the efficiency is the least known factor, but it shows that no additional energy source is needed.
