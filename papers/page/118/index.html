William Alexander Aldred · October 31, 2026

Trautman's own review calls the aligned estimate "(unrealistic)". No derivation of the coherence from the matter sector has ever been supplied, by Kopczyński's programme or by mine: in my own corpus's census of dead ends, the coherence of the spin density σ at the crossing is a premise of the frame, not an output, and it is printed that way. This paper asks the question my problem board poses in its cheapest form — whether drag-accretion onto a twinned, spinning seed aligns what it gathers — and answers it completely, in the negative, with the price attached. I prove a precession-invariance theorem: the torsion torque I own, ds/dt = Ω(n̂ × s), preserves every spin's angle to the seed axis identically, so owned torsion cannot align at any strength. I derive a seed-coherence dilution law, C_rms = √(N_0² + M)/(N_0 + M): a perfectly coherent seed falls to C ≈ 0.011 after growth by a factor of 100, so coherence must be re-made continuously or lost. The Relaxation That Empties the Universe

William Alexander Aldred · October 31, 2026

In 1985 Larry Abbott built the best relaxation the cosmological constant problem has ever had. A scalar field sitting in a washboard potential — a long slope cut by small, regular steps — can tunnel from step to step, and each tunneling lowers the effective cosmological constant a little. Keep stepping, and a vacuum energy that starts out huge walks itself down toward zero in small increments, with no one having to set it small by hand at the start. I price that mechanism in this paper at full strength, because it deserves it: it is a genuine dynamical relaxation, and it was the strongest counter-proposal Weinberg's 1989 survey had to answer. Then I price where it stops, also at full strength. The ball does not stop on the step we need. It rolls past every interesting value and keeps going, and the universe it delivers at the bottom is empty and curvature-dominated — no matter worth naming, no structure, nothing for a cosmology to be about. To make it stop where matter still exists, the step structure itself has to be tuned so the last useful step lands inside the narrow window we observe. The tuning the mechanism was built to remove comes back in through the back door, wearing the steps. My program meets Abbott from a different direction. In my frame the universe's vacuum value is not relaxed toward over cosmic time at all; it is read at a crossing — at the sever, at the seam where the bounce crosses — the way you read a number off a gauge at the moment the needle passes, not the way you wait for a tank to drain to the right line. This paper states exactly what that re-timing is and exactly what it is not. It is not a stopping rule. It derives no stopping rule, and it claims none. It is not an answer to Abbott, and it does not refute him: Abbott's problem — derive the stop — is not solved by declining to relax, and I say so in §3 in those words. What the paper does is put the price where my own books can see it. Abbott stands as the standing proof that dynamics alone does not stop, and that proof is why my program's own unproduced toll must never be quietly replaced by any relaxation-shaped hope. In 1967 and 1968, Ya. B. That is the original statement of the vacuum catastrophe, before the catastrophe had the name, and in my judgment it is still its hardest form — because it does not depend on any new physics being right. It depends only on the fields already on the books refusing to add up to the number on the sky. For fifty years the standard exit from that debt was supersymmetry: an exact supersymmetry cancels the fluctuation sum, partner against partner, and a supersymmetry broken at the TeV scale leaves, in the census's accounting, "only" about sixty orders of magnitude unexplained. The Null That Can Be Permanent

William Alexander Aldred · October 31, 2026

Imagine a door you can knock on forever. You knock, and no one answers. You knock again, louder, with better instruments, for decades, and still no one answers. What have you learned? In most of science, silence that deep means the room is empty. But if the room has a place in it where a person could stand and never be heard, then the silence proves nothing either way. That is the situation of neutrinoless double-beta decay, and it is the dead end this paper prices. The question underneath the experiment is a binary fact about nature: is the neutrino its own antiparticle, a Majorana particle, or a Dirac particle, distinct from its antiparticle? The flagship test built to answer it watches for a decay that can happen only if the Majorana answer is true. The KamLAND-Zen Collaboration, with its complete dataset, has now watched to extraordinary depth and found nothing: a half-life bound beyond 3.8 in units of ten to the twenty-sixth years at 90 percent confidence, with the effective Majorana mass in 28 to 122 meV, as published in Physical Review Letters 135, 262501 (2025). I credit that null at full strength: it is a measurement, not a failure, and it closes real ground. But in the normal ordering, the effective Majorana mass can cancel toward zero even if neutrinos are Majorana particles. The observable has a blind spot, and the answer may live exactly in it; the experiment can return null forever without the question being answered. My census treats this limit at its floor, and my paper The Unpaired Face treats pair-unity at the weak sector's missing-partner address. In 2003, Arvind Borde, Alan Guth, and Alexander Vilenkin proved the cosmological version of that plain fact, and they proved it as a theorem, not as an opinion: any geodesic along which the average expansion is positive is past-incomplete. Programs speak of eternal cycles — oscillation without a first turn, expansion and contraction forever in both directions — while owing the theorem an account of their geodesics' average expansion, an account almost none of them files. All the Way Down: A Handedness Count on the Axis

William Alexander Aldred · November 1, 2026 · 8 pp

I said the spin set at our universe's birth scar should cascade through every scale, and that it would line up somewhere. The scar has a published address: the axis the cosmic microwave background's largest patterns line up on. So the test is simple. Count spiral galaxies by handedness on the two sides of that axis and see if the count lines up. The test was written down and sealed before the catalogue was opened: one axis, one statistic, one bar. I counted 77,840 galaxies with a published spin direction in an author-posted Sloan Digital Sky Survey catalogue. In the hemisphere the axis points into, clockwise galaxies lead by 1.23 percent.

The Eight, Vibrating

William Alexander Aldred · November 1, 2026 · 6 pp

I said I see vibrating strings of light and sound, twisting together in a figure eight. The static figure eight was built and computed before, and it died by theorem: a frozen defect network carries no Nieh–Yan charge, and the charge is zero by the shape of the forms, not by smallness. But I never described a frozen eight. Mine moves. So this paper builds the moving one. Two screw strands cross at the eight's own node angle, ninety degrees, and their centres vibrate against each other along the normal — the twisting mode. I compute the Nieh–Yan charge over a full period of the motion. It is zero.

William Alexander Aldred · November 1, 2026 · 6 pp

Paper #146 priced a quiet problem in seismology: on one fixed global catalog, the three standard estimators of the Gutenberg–Richter b-value disagree with each other by 0.171 — nearly eight times the largest formal error any of them quotes. That catalog was used the way the service ships it, aftershocks and all, and #146 said so on its face. This paper asks the obvious next question. Is some of that spread the aftershocks' fault? Take one dense regional catalog — the USGS Japan box, 2014–2023, 3,556 events at magnitude 4.5 and above — decluster it by a standard method whose windows were fixed in advance, and run the same three estimators on both versions.
