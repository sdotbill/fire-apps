Pocket Geometry and the Warp Energy Bill: Van den Broeck Reduction Arithmetic

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24147903 Van den Broeck's modification keeps a warp bubble's internal radius at one hundred metres while shrinking the radius the outside universe sees, and with it the radius that enters the energy integral. Because the integral scales as the external radius squared, a neck of one centimetre cuts the requirement from 1.01e+47 joules to 1.01e+39 joules, and a ten-micrometre neck cuts it to 1.01e+33 joules, a mass equivalent of 1.12e+16 kilograms, with a reduction factor of 1.0e-14. This is the one published reshaping that truly changes the arithmetic, and this paper states its achievement at full size. The verdict nonetheless remains negative: the residual bill is still negative energy in quantities no source supplies, and the neck's own geometry concentrates the gradients the integral was spared from counting. Computed, not measured.

How Long a Warp Bubble Can Persist: Crossing Times and the Quantum-Inequality Clock

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24147882 Three clocks govern a warp bubble of radius one hundred metres: light crosses the bubble in 3.34e-07 seconds and a one-metre wall in 3.34e-09 seconds, while the quantum-inequality sampling bound, evaluated schematically, licenses wall-density negative energy for about 2.62e+08 seconds at one-metre wall thickness, 1.48e+02 seconds at ten to the minus five metres, and 8.30e-05 seconds at ten to the minus ten metres. The licence therefore exceeds the bubble's own crossing time by fifteen orders of magnitude for the thick wall and still exceeds it at the thinnest tabulated wall, crossing below it only on extrapolation near a picometre. The verdict is deliberately two-sided: persistence is not the thick-wall bubble's problem, since its energy is, and persistence becomes binding only in the extreme thin-wall corner, where it adds an independent exclusion to a region already excluded on other grounds. All times are computed schematically from stated assumptions, not measured.

Stopping a Warp Bubble: The Horizon Problem and the Deceleration Budget

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24147954 Formation is only half of any journey. This paper examines the other half for a warp bubble of one hundred metres radius: how it stops. At ship speeds above light speed a horizon separates the interior from the forward wall, so no signal from the occupants can reach the part of the geometry that must change for the bubble to slow; the signal lag across the bubble, 3.34e-07 seconds at light speed, becomes infinite in effect beyond it. The energy that must be withdrawn to stop equals the formation budget, 1.01e+47 joules at the headline wall thickness, and it must be withdrawn by agents outside the bubble, along the route, acting on a schedule laid down before departure. The verdict is plain: a superluminal warp bubble is not a vehicle but a projectile, launched rather than flown, and any route it travels must be prepared as a track with the stopping machinery built in advance. Computed schematically, not measured.

Warp-Bubble Wall Thickness: The Shear Ceiling and the Quantum Inequality Squeeze From Opposite Sides

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24147870 A warp-bubble wall is squeezed from two directions. From one side, the velocity gradient across the wall implies a shear rate of order the ship speed divided by the wall thickness, and a maximum shear rate of 4.04e+24 per second, derived from the spin-torsion density ceiling, forbids any wall thinner than 7.43e-17 metres. From the other side, the Ford-Roman quantum inequality licenses negative energy at warp-wall densities only for walls of order one hundred Planck lengths, 1.62e-33 metres, as Pfenning and Ford established. The two demands miss each other by sixteen orders of magnitude: at the quantum-legal thickness the shear exceeds the ceiling by a factor of 4.59e+16. The verdict is plain: there is no wall thickness at which a light-speed warp bubble satisfies both the shear ceiling and the quantum inequality, and the exclusion needs no appeal to the bubble's total energy at all.
