Baryon Isocurvature Limits on an Asymmetry Generated at a Bounce

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142485 If the baryon asymmetry was generated with slightly different efficiency in different places, the universe carries baryon isocurvature perturbations: fluctuations in the baryon-to-photon ratio at fixed total density. Microwave-background data bound such fluctuations tightly. This paper translates an isocurvature fraction bound of beta_iso < 0.038 into an allowed root-mean-square scatter in the asymmetry, finding S_rms below about 4.02e-04 under the stated translation, and tabulates which production scatters pass: a patch-to-patch efficiency scatter of 1e-3 or 1e-4 passes comfortably, while scatters of 0.05 and above do not, with the 1e-2 case recorded as passing = False under the stated limit. A bounce that generates the asymmetry through a spatially varying process, such as inhomogeneous reheating of the bounce surface, must therefore be homogeneous in its efficiency at the sub-percent level. Computed from stated assumptions, not measured.

Cold and Nonthermal Production After a Bounce That Reheats Gently

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142520 Not every bounce reheats the universe to extreme temperatures. If the post-bounce reheating temperature is low, thermal production of the heavy particles that baryogenesis usually relies on is shut off, and the asymmetry must be made cold or nonthermally. This paper computes the trade-off. Thermally, a reheating temperature T_reh permits heavy-neutrino production only up to masses near 3 T_reh, so T_reh = 1e9 GeV caps thermal leptogenesis at 3.0e+09 GeV, below its usual minimum scale. Nonthermally, decays of a heavy field of mass 1e13 GeV with branching ratio 0.1 produce eta = 3.74e-12 at T_reh = 1e9 GeV and 3.74e-09 at 1e12 GeV, for reference CP asymmetry 1e-6 and efficiency 0.1; matching the observed eta at the reference parameters requires T_reh near 1.63e+11 GeV. Low-temperature bounces are therefore not excluded from baryogenesis, but they must use nonthermal production and they pay for it in parameter tuning. Computed from stated assumptions, not measured.

Electroweak Baryogenesis at a Bounce: A Window the Bounce Does Not Open

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142425 Electroweak baryogenesis needs a strongly first-order electroweak phase transition, quantified by v_c/T_c >= 1, which the Standard Model does not provide at the measured Higgs mass. A hopeful suggestion is that a bounce cosmology might alter the expansion rate at the transition enough to change the outcome. This paper tests the suggestion with numbers and rejects it. At 100 GeV the radiation density is 1.53e+28 kg per cubic metre, a fraction 1.57e-30 of the torsion density ceiling; the corresponding correction to the expansion rate is of order 1.25e-15. No plausible reading of so small a correction converts a crossover into a strong transition, and the washout-avoidance condition remains exactly as it is in the standard history. The negative result is stated plainly: in this framework the bounce neither rescues electroweak baryogenesis nor damages it, and the mechanism stands or falls on particle physics, as before. Computed from stated assumptions, not measured.

Entropy Production at the Bounce and the Dilution of an Earlier Asymmetry

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142398 If a bounce releases entropy, every relic abundance produced beforehand is diluted by the same factor, and the baryon asymmetry is no exception. This paper computes how much entropy production a pre-bounce asymmetry can survive. With the yield from any mechanism bounded by Y_pre <= epsilon/g_star <= 9.37e-03 for CP asymmetry at most unity, and the required final yield fixed at 8.69e-11, the largest tolerable entropy factor is Delta = 1.08e+08. A factor of 100 already demands a pre-bounce yield of 8.69e-09; an initial yield of 1e-8 emerges as 1.00e-09 after a factor of ten and as 1.00e-11 after a factor of a thousand, the latter well below the observed value. Bounces with entropy factors above about 1e5 therefore erase any plausible pre-bounce asymmetry and force baryogenesis to occur on the expanding side. Computed from stated assumptions, not measured.

Grand-Unified Baryogenesis Checked Against Proton Stability

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142438 Grand-unified theories violate baryon number through heavy gauge bosons, and their decays are a classic baryogenesis channel, but the same bosons mediate proton decay, which has never been observed. This paper computes both sides of that tension on one page. The proton lifetime scales as the fourth power of the heavy boson mass: at M_X = 1e15 GeV the estimate is 4.60e+31 years, excluded by the experimental bound of 2.4e34 years; at 1e16 GeV it is 4.60e+35 years, and at 1e17 GeV, 4.60e+39 years. In a bounce history the high production temperature is available on both sides of the bounce, but only expanding-side production escapes subsequent washout and dilution. Gravitational Baryogenesis at the Bounce, Where the Ricci Scalar Changes Fastest

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142509 Gravitational baryogenesis couples the derivative of the Ricci scalar to the baryon current, so that a changing spacetime curvature acts as a chemical potential for baryon number. The coupling is negligible in a slowly evolving universe and largest where curvature changes fastest: at a bounce. This paper computes the effect with the curvature scale set by the torsion density ceiling. The maximum expansion rate is H_max = 2.33e+24 per second, or 1.53e+00 GeV, giving a curvature-derivative scale Rdot of 3.61e+00 GeV^3. The mechanism is therefore viable at a bounce only with a suppression scale well below the Planck mass, a requirement that any concrete model must justify. How Much Lepton Asymmetry Can the Neutrino Seas Hold?

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142469 Lepton number need not reside in baryons or charged leptons; it can be stored as a chemical potential in the cosmic neutrino seas, where it is nearly invisible. This paper computes the storage capacity and its observable side effects. A degeneracy parameter xi = mu/T of 0.1 in each flavour stores a lepton yield per flavour of 1.45e-02, vastly more than the baryon yield, while shifting the effective number of relativistic species by only 0.013 and the helium mass fraction to 0.226 if the electron flavour shares the asymmetry. Storing the entire observed asymmetry equivalent in neutrinos would need only xi of order 5.98e-10. A bounce history passes the neutrino seas through decoupling twice, but storage established after the final decoupling is what observations constrain. Computed from stated assumptions, not measured.

One Asymmetry, Two Measurements: Nucleosynthesis and Microwave-Background Consistency

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142532 The baryon-to-photon ratio is measured twice, by unrelated physics separated by hundreds of thousands of years: by the light-element abundances from the first minutes and by the acoustic peaks of the microwave background from year 380,000. This paper checks that a single value satisfies both, which is the final consistency gate any baryogenesis mechanism, in any cosmology, must pass. At the microwave-background value eta = 6.12e-10, the predicted primordial deuterium abundance is D/H = 2.60e-05, against the observed 2.53e-05; the predicted helium mass fraction is 0.247. Shifting eta to 5.8e-10 raises deuterium to 2.83e-05, and 6.6e-10 lowers it to 2.30e-05, both outside the tight observational interval. A bounce between production and nucleosynthesis would show up here as a mismatch, because entropy production after production but before the first minutes changes eta between the two measurements; the observed agreement bounds any such late entropy release tightly. Sakharov's Three Conditions Evaluated in a Contracting Phase

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142354 Sakharov's conditions for generating a baryon asymmetry are usually checked in an expanding universe. This paper checks them, with numbers, in the contracting phase that precedes a bounce. Baryon-number violation is supplied by electroweak sphaleron-like rates, Gamma_sph = 25 alpha_w^5 T with alpha_w = 1/30. The ratio of this rate to the magnitude of the Hubble rate is 7.32e+09 at 100 GeV and 7.32e+01 at 1e10 GeV; the two rates are equal near T = 7.32e+11 GeV. Departure from equilibrium, the third condition, behaves differently under contraction: processes fall out of equilibrium as the temperature rises toward the bounce rather than as it falls, so the ordering of production and freeze-out is reversed relative to the expanding case. The first two conditions, baryon violation and CP violation, are properties of the particle physics and are unchanged by the sign of the expansion. The conclusion is that a contracting phase can satisfy all three conditions, but any asymmetry it produces must then run the gauntlet of the bounce itself. Computed, not measured.

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

Washout Survival of a Baryon Asymmetry Across the Hot Phase of a Bounce

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24142342 An asymmetry produced before or during the hot phase of a bouncing cosmology is partially erased by the same interactions that created it, a process called washout. This paper computes the surviving fraction of an initial baryon yield as a function of a washout strength K and of the duration of the hot phase, which a bounce can lengthen. For weak washout, K = 0.1, the surviving fraction is 0.976; at K = 1 it is 0.785; at K = 10 it falls to 8.84e-02 for a standard hot phase and to 7.81e-03 when the bounce triples that phase; at K = 100 essentially nothing survives, with a fraction of 2.91e-11. The integrated washout at K = 10 is W = 2.43 e-foldings of depletion. The practical result is a survival boundary: production mechanisms operating in the strong-washout regime cannot rely on an asymmetry made before the bounce, whatever its initial size within reasonable bounds. Computed from stated assumptions, not measured.
