Gamma-Ray Budgets from Dwarf Spheroidal Galaxies: Annihilation Fluxes, Astrophysical Factors and the Cross Section a Stacked Search Can Reach

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24151911 This study computes gamma-ray fluxes expected from annihilation in dwarf spheroidal galaxies, using published astrophysical factors for the best-measured systems, and derives the velocity-averaged cross section that a stacked search can reach. The flux separates cleanly into a particle factor, set by mass, cross section and photon yield, and an astrophysical factor set by the dark matter distribution along the line of sight. For a 100 GeV candidate annihilating to bottom quarks, the brightest dwarfs give fluxes that place the reachable cross section close to the value expected for a thermal relic, which is why the stacked dwarf search is the sharpest indirect test in that mass range. Uncertainties in the astrophysical factors, not photon statistics, set the width of the result. All results are computed, not measured.

Paper · Dark-matter detection

Capture of Dark Matter in the Earth and the Heat-Flow Bound: What Terrestrial Heating Already Excludes

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24151925 This study computes capture of halo dark matter in the Earth and converts the resulting annihilation power into a heat-flow budget that can be compared with the measured terrestrial heat output of about 44 terawatts. Capture in the Earth is resonantly enhanced when the candidate mass matches the masses of abundant elements, especially iron, oxygen and silicon, and is otherwise far weaker than solar capture because of the smaller mass and escape speed of the Earth. Even at the resonant masses, cross sections already excluded by underground detectors would be needed for annihilation heating to approach one per cent of the measured heat flow, so terrestrial heating is a consistency check rather than a competitive limit at present sensitivity. The calculation states exactly where that ordering would reverse. All results are computed, not measured.

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

Inelastic Dark Matter Scattering: Kinematic Suppression, Maximum Splittings and the Targets That Keep a Split Candidate Visible

William Alexander Aldred · November 11, 2026

DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.24151978 This study computes the kinematics of inelastic scattering, in which the incoming candidate converts to a slightly heavier state, and derives the maximum mass splitting accessible for each target and candidate mass. The splitting raises the minimum speed required for a recoil of given energy, so scattering is suppressed by the falling tail of the halo velocity distribution and can vanish entirely when the required speed exceeds the galactic escape speed in the Earth frame. Heavy targets and heavy candidates tolerate larger splittings; a splitting of 100 keV is accessible in xenon for candidates near 100 GeV but is closed for light targets and light candidates. Inelastic kinematics naturally produces strong annual modulation and target-dependent rates, which are signatures, not inconveniences. All results are computed, not measured.
