What a Litre of Vacuum Would Weigh: Mass Equivalents of Zero-Point Densities From a Bottle to the Observable Universe

William Alexander Aldred · November 10, 2026

Converted to mass equivalents, a litre of vacuum carries about 6 x 10^-30 kg at the observed density, about 5 x 10^24 kg at the electroweak-cutoff estimate, and about 3 x 10^91 kg at the Planck-cutoff estimate; the Planck-cutoff vacuum in the observable universe's volume would outweigh its entire critical-density mass-energy budget by a factor of about 10^121.
