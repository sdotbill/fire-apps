The Aldred Limit: Pinning the Einstein–Cartan Spin–Density Bound — A bottom-up derivation, a prescription verdict, and a falsifiable claim

by William Alexander Aldred — Independent Researcher

Published October 20, 2026 · Zenodo · 7 pp · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.23485325

Abstract

In Einstein–Cartan (EC) gravity, the spin of fermionic matter sources spacetime torsion, and eliminating torsion through the Cartan equation produces an effective energy density ρ_eff = ρ − κ²⟨S²⟩ in which the spin term opposes compression. We derive the resulting density bound bottom-up, starting from a Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff solver verified against the published SLy equation of state, and we pin the bound rather than quote it to an order of magnitude: under the incoherent (randomly oriented) spin prescription of Böhmer et al., the bound is ρ_A = ρ_Pl (m/m_Pl)²/(8π·coef), which for neutron matter gives a band 2.4×10^54–9.7×10^54 g cm⁻³ depending on the density convention adopted, with headline value ρ_A ≈ 10^55 g cm⁻³. This is the Kopczyński–Trautman bounce density of 1972–73; it is not new, and we claim no novelty for the bound itself.

What is established here is (i) the bottom-up derivation and pinning, (ii) a numerical argument, at the level of the channel estimates of Section 3.2, that the incoherent spin branch is not a modeling choice but is physically forced at bounce conditions, (iii) the equivalence of the density bound with a torsion (“twist”) bound T_A ≈ 1.3×10^16 m⁻¹ through the Cartan equation, and (iv) an exact analysis of the author's earlier entropy-form saturation ansatz against the EC effective density, which fails as a map at the first correction term but calibrates to ρ_Aldred = ρ_A/2.

The falsifiable claim of this paper is: fermion spin caps gravitational compression at ρ_A ≈ 10^55 g cm⁻³ (equivalently T_A ≈ 10^16 m⁻¹), replacing singularities with bounces; and if the progenitor rotates, the galaxy-spin dipole axis and the CMB Cold Spot direction must coincide — a claim that dies to any consistent counterexample above ρ_A, or to the axis failing to coincide with the Cold Spot at catalogue precision. The axis half of that claim has had its first catalogue-level test, reported in Section 7: on the largest public catalogues the registered kill line is not executable at the required precision, and the rotating extension stands neither confirmed nor killed.
