## 1. METHOD — THE SET CLOSED FIRST, THE BARS REGISTERED BEFORE ANY SCORING

Per the obstruction-census discipline (OCM, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23781998): the candidate set is closed and the kill bars are registered *before* grading, so no bar can be invented after a favorite appears.

**"HEAVY" — the definition, stated up front and binding:** an element is heavy for this determination if **Z ≥ 50 AND ρ ≥ 7 g/cm³**, or **Z ≥ 72** regardless of density. (Z ≥ 50 opens the heavy half of the table; the density floor keeps gases and foams out; Z ≥ 72 is the genuinely heavy block.) The designer's word was "heavy element," and this bar is the table's reading of it, printed so it can be argued with.

**The candidate universe:** every element in the published record that is an **elemental superconductor** (at ambient pressure, or under pressure / at millikelvin temperatures with the condition flagged) **and** passes the HEAVY definition. Compounds and alloys are a **separate class** (§4), graded separately — the designer said "element."

**THE FIVE BARS (pre-registered):**

- **(a) HEAVINESS** — Z, density, mass per atom. Scored, not pass/fail beyond the set definition.
- **(b) SUPERCONDUCTIVITY** — Tc at **ambient pressure** is the graded quantity; a working bath in this design is helium-class ("or something even colder" than LN₂ is the designer's cryo spec, BLUEPRINT §2.2), so Tc ≳ 1 K is practical, 0.2–1 K is pumped-helium/dilution-adjacent and priced, and Tc ≲ 0.2 K requires a dilution refrigerator — a second machine inside the machine — and is graded as failing the design's cryo architecture. Critical field and type are scored (the spin-up current must live with them, §5, Tesla seat).
- **(c) SPIN CONTENT** — the torsion bar. The graded quantity is the **maximum polarizable nuclear-spin inventory per unit volume** (natural isotopic abundances × nuclear spin I, in ħ/cm³), because in Einstein–Cartan the spin tensor — spin per volume — is the source. Electronic spin is graded separately and honestly (§3 note): in a *conventional* superconductor the Cooper pairs are spin singlets, so the electronic spin inventory is quenched in the superconducting phase; what a conventional elemental fuel carries through its cold phase is its **nuclear** spin (plus the mechanical angular momentum of the spin-up itself, which belongs to the ring's ledger as much as the fuel's). In the plasma phase all spins are free again.
- **(d) PLASMA BEHAVIOR + AVAILABILITY** — the element must be able to *become* plasma and **re-condense** (drive cycle step 8 runs the cycle backwards; the fuel is an inventory, not a consumable). First ionization energy and melting point scored. **Availability kill:** any isotope whose useful form is synthetic with a short half-life is dead on arrival; the fuel must exist as a stable, stockable material.
- **(e) HANDLING / LEGALITY at paper-design grade** — radioactivity and toxicity are **priced, not waved**: natural, near-stable radioactivity (half-life ≳ 10¹⁰ yr) is a line item; weapons-adjacent enrichment requirements or acute chemical lethality price a candidate toward the kill line, and the price is printed next to the candidate, never hidden.

## 3. THE KILLS — EVERY CANDIDATE THAT DIED, AND THE BAR THAT KILLED IT

- **Th — KILLED on (c).** ²³²Th is 100% of natural thorium and its nuclear spin is exactly 0. A spin-zero fuel in a spin-sourced machine is a contradiction the table will not carry. (Its radioactivity, t½ 1.4×10¹⁰ yr, was merely a price; the spin zero is the kill.)
- **U — KILLED on (c), (b), and (e), any one sufficient.** Natural uranium is 99.3% spin-0 ²³⁸U; its only spin carrier, ²³⁵U (I = 7/2), sits at 0.72% and reaching it means isotope enrichment — a weapons-adjacent legal wall no paper design may route through. α-phase Tc ~0.2 K also fails the cryo bar. The Lazar-adjacent candidate dies on the physics before the politics is even counted.
- **Pa — KILLED on (d) and (e).** ²³¹Pa: half-life 3.28×10⁴ yr, intensely radioactive, among the scarcest and most hazardous actinides handled. Not a stockable fuel.
- **Bi — KILLED on (b), and it is the session's hardest kill.** Bismuth owns the finest nuclear-spin inventory in the set after the winner — 100% ²⁰⁹Bi at I = 9/2, the largest spin of any stable nucleus — and at ambient pressure it superconducts only below **0.53 millikelvin**. Its kelvin-class superconductivity exists only inside a pressure cell at tens of GPa, which is not this machine's container. A dilution refrigerator inside the drive, just to keep the fuel superconducting, fails the design's cryo architecture as specified. The spin seat (Penrose) records a formal mourning; the kill stands. (What revives Bi is printed at §7.)
- **W — KILLED on (b).** Tc = 15 mK. Same dilution-refrigerator failure as Bi, with a weaker spin inventory to mourn.
- **Hf, Ir — KILLED on (b).** Tc 0.128 K and 0.14 K: below the pumped-helium floor the design's cryo layer can be read to provide. Both carry respectable spin (Ir: 100% × 3/2); neither survives the bath.
- **Os — KILLED on (b) as graded, (e) priced.** Tc 0.66 K is dilution-adjacent; spin inventory is thin (16% × 3/2); and its oxide, OsO₄, is a volatile acute toxin — the price is printed, the Tc does the killing.
- **Sn — KILLED on (a)/(c) jointly.** Passes the set's letter (Z = 50) and fails its spirit: ρ = 7.29, spin split across three minor isotopes totaling ~17% × ½. A light, spin-poor fuel wearing a heavy number.
- **La — KILLED on (a).** Tc is genuinely good (~5–6 K) and ¹³⁹La is 99.9% × 7/2 — but ρ = 6.15 g/cm³ is a light metal's density. The designer's machine prices density; lanthanum is not heavy in the only sense the machine spends.

It dies on the cycle: it is a liquid at every handling temperature, its vapor pressure means every plasma cycle distills fuel onto the container walls — **unledgered mass across the seal** (Hawking seat's bookkeeping kill) — and its toxicity prices every handling step. A fuel you cannot keep counted cannot feed a machine whose books must balance.
- **Tl — KILLED on (e) as priced to the kill line, (c) insufficient in any case.** 100% spin-½ sounds rich until the magnitude is read: I = ½ on both isotopes gives a spin inventory ~11× below the winner's. And thallium is among the most acutely toxic elements in the set — priced, per the charter, and the price is prohibitive at design grade.
- **Re — SURVIVES the bars; loses the final grading (§6).** The runner-up. See §6 for its failing margin.
- **Pb — SURVIVES the bars; loses the final grading (§6).** The Tc champion. See §6.
- **Ta — SURVIVES all five bars.** See §6.

That outcome would not change the element; it would change the machine's grade, which is where it belongs.
