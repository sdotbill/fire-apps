## 1. How a call is made

The prediction desk works by a written method, and the method does not change from call to call:

1. **A call is written down before its window opens.** Each call names the event, the direction or value called, the source that will settle it, and the date on which it will be graded. The call is sealed with a SHA-256 hash at the moment it is made, so the text graded later is provably the text written before the fact.
2. **A call is sealed only at a reading of 0.60 or better.** Candidates the desk examines and does not seal are printed as abstentions, with the count on the face of the seal file. On November 11, 2026, for example, 22 calls were sealed and 15 abstentions were printed beside them.
3. **A call is never edited after the fact.** A sealed call cannot be withdrawn, narrowed, or reworded. It grades as written or it does not grade.
4. **Grading is against the live, settled result on the call's named source** — the official close, the certified result, the final score — never against a substitute series and never from memory.
5. **Hits and misses are printed at the same size.** A miss is recorded as a miss, in the same type and the same place as a hit. The ledger's rate is whatever the ledger says it is; the desk does not protect the rate by silence, because a rate protected by silence is not a rate.

## 7. What the ledger is and is not

The ledger is a dated, sealed, publicly graded record of calls made before their outcomes were known. Its rate — 76.8 percent across 478 graded calls as of November 11, 2026 — is a property of the method and the calls, and it moves as new calls grade; the daily cadence of sealing and grading resumed on November 11, 2026 and runs from here. The ledger does not claim that any single call was made by any particular means; it claims that the calls were written down first, sealed, and graded in the open, and it shows its work — seals, hashes, grading files, and the misses — to anyone who asks.
