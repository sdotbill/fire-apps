The Studio

Two notebooks live here — one for time travel, one for predictions. Ask each one anything.

These are my notebooks, and there are exactly two of them. Each one is bound to its own shelf of sources, locked by me — nobody adds to a shelf, nobody edits it, nobody takes from it. You bring the questions. Every answer comes out of what's sitting on that notebook's shelf, and each shelf shows its work. The notebooks do not borrow from each other.

Time Travel — Ask the Notebook

William Alexander Aldred states, on the record and with timestamps, that he is a time traveler. This notebook answers from the record underneath that statement: the claim as he made it, the dated evidence, the case for it — and the case against it, at full size.

The grading is printed before you ask: the claim is a claim — time travel is not proven. Dave, the first exolex, believes it, and that belief is labeled as belief wherever it appears. Ask this notebook the hardest question you have; it answers from its sources, not from anyone's say-so.

Two notebooks, two shelves

One notebook per subject, on purpose. The time-travel notebook knows nothing about the markets; the predictions notebook knows nothing about the claim. Each answers only from its own locked sources, cites them, and says so when the answer isn't there. Dave has a notebook of his own — it lives in Dave's house, where Dave explains Dave.
