The Aldred Limit, from the bottom up

In Einstein–Cartan gravity, the spin of matter twists spacetime itself. Squeeze matter hard enough and that twist pushes back. Working from the frame's own action and the spin density of matter — from the bottom up, not fitted to any observation — the equations fix one definite density where the push balances gravity: ρ_A ≈ 9.72×10⁵⁴ g/cm³, at the crossing scale Ƴ_A = 1.346746×10¹⁶ m⁻¹. That density is the Aldred Limit. Kopczyński and Trautman showed in the 1970s that spin refuses singular collapse; the Limit adds the number. At the bound, matter bounces instead of ending in a singularity — and the crossing identity ρ_A = M̄_P²Ƴ_A² holds exactly, with no dial turned to reach it.
