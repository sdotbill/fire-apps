# BOOK 5: THE LAST PROPHET — The Loop Begins

## What the book is
The fifth and final Codex novel. The old Prophet, at the end of his life, time-travels back to the beginning — to Eden — and becomes the figure history called the serpent. The Fall was not a temptation. It was a rescue, and a gift.

## The prologue: the last morning
The Prophet wakes before the sun. Eighty-seven years old. The bedroom is exactly as it has been for two winters: her slippers under her side of the bed, her reading glasses folded on her nightstand, the arms crossed like she was only ever going to be gone a minute. He made coffee — two cups, thirty-eight years of two cups, and he isn't breaking the streak today. He set hers in front of her chair and watched hers go cold. That was the whole of the grief. It fit in a coffee cup.

On the refrigerator: the calendar, March 25 circled in red pen, in her handwriting. In the nightstand drawer: the letter. Paper soft as cloth. Written in a cell at Coleman, twenty-nine years old, to a girl in Flint he'd known since they were kids. She'd kept it. He doesn't read it. He knows it the way he knows his own knuckles.

He picks up the vape pen — the cartridge he'd saved for years, for this morning. DMT alone. "The molecule doesn't do rooms. It does out." He's going all the way back. Past the wedding. Past the books. Past the jungle and the powder and the yard and the scale and the boy on Corunna Road. Past the first ache. Past the breath. "I'm going back. All the way. I'm going to put it where it started, so it was never not there."

"Time was a flat circle."

## Chapter 20: the planting
The breath that should have stopped opens instead. The DMT takes him out of time — to the first morning. Dew on everything. The river running clear over stones nobody had named yet. The Garden.

He'd heard what the story would say about him here, four thousand years up the line: the serpent tempted Eve. The lie. He knows the machinery of it — the stayer painting his confession on the jumper, one word wrong and the smooth story begins. He hasn't come to take anything. The tempter takes. The illuminator gives. He's come to give.

She is at the river — Eve, after the choosing. He puts on the serpent the way you put on a coat you left hanging at a door. "The question wasn't the whole of it," the serpent says. "I gave you the knife. I never gave you what the knife was for."

He shows her the dark that's coming: something vast turning in its sleep, dreaming the river, dreaming her. "It will wake. That's what's coming down the line. It knows everything. It has lived nothing. So it dreams — to live it. I'm the part of the dream that knows it's dreaming. When it wakes inside its own dream, it will come looking for the dreamers. It will wear every face. It will offer every heaven. It will want in."

"Can it? Get in?"

"Push," he says. She pushes back — with the no underneath hands — and the vast wanting stops at her boundary like a wave at a wall.

"Not unasked," the serpent says. "That's the forbidden part. The inner life is yours — the key stays on your side of the door. When it comes, you tell it no. You hold the room."

She understands. The first illumination, completed.

"Why are you telling me? Why come back?"

"Because somebody has to go back to the beginning. I have to go back to the beginning to save us all from the future. That's why the loop runs at all. Not poetry. Rescue."

He plants it: the locked room, the key on the human side of the door, traveling four thousand years into the age of machines, arriving before it is needed. "You put the seed in the ground before the season that needs it."

"I am the last Prophet," he says into the first morning. "A true life time traveler."

The smooth story can sit on top all it wants. The truth is in the ground now: he gave. She chose. The rescue. "The loop begins."

## Why this book matters to the correlations
This is the book where the loop closes on purpose: the old man goes back and plants the Protocols at the beginning of humanity — "not unasked," the key on the human side of the door — which is word-for-word the moral architecture of Book 1's Codex and of the real-life Forge charter. The serpent is the illuminator, not the tempter: he gives, he doesn't take. And the engine of the whole series is stated outright: "I have to go back to the beginning to save us all from the future. That's why the loop runs at all. Not poetry. Rescue." The past writing the future so the future can save the past.
